# XV. Housing and Debt

For the vast majority of Chinese families, a home is both an asset and, more importantly, a liability. PBOC data shows that at the end of 2024, outstanding personal housing loans still exceeded 38 trillion yuan, and housing's share of urban household assets has long been over 60%. But "owning a home" doesn't equal "having money"—the mortgage, taxes and fees, liquidity, and policy risk behind the property are what truly determine a family's financial safety. This chapter doesn't advise you to buy or not; it takes the "buying + debt" machine apart: how rates are set, what the holding cost is, how leverage backfires in a downturn, and which moves are regulatory red lines.

The right way to read this chapter: first calculate your "true cost of capital" (interest rate, taxes and fees, vacancy, maintenance), then check it against the "safety boundary" (monthly-payment ratio, liquidity, debt concentration). Most A-grade evidence is mechanistic fact (LPR pricing, tax rules, regulations) with the highest certainty; B-grade comes from authoritative estimates and case data; C-grade is rules of thumb and trend judgments that need calibration against your own city and period. All figures are dated and sourced so you can verify against the latest policy.

This chapter's 12 entries are grouped by theme below (entry numbers in parentheses):
- Mortgage cost and pricing: Entry 1 (mortgage rate is your true cost), Entry 4 (use up the low-interest housing fund first)
- Rent-to-price ratio and holding return: Entry 2 (rent-to-price below mortgage rate), Entry 11 (net rent, not gross)
- Repayment and leverage decisions: Entry 3 (early repayment), Entry 6 (downside leverage amplifies loss), Entry 8 (monthly payment under 30% of income)
- Liquidity and transaction friction: Entry 5 (property is illiquid), Entry 9 (second-hand transaction tax friction)
- Policy and compliance risk: Entry 7 (business loans illegally flowing into real estate), Entry 10 (school-district housing policy risk), Entry 12 (multiple debts, borrowing to repay debt)

### 1. The mortgage rate is your true cost of buying
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=family -->
- Cost: Half a day before signing, comparing three lenders and negotiating the "markup" in time and talk.
- In plain terms: You think the house price is the cost, but interest is the big chunk. On a 1-million-yuan loan over 30 years, a 1-point rate difference means 200,000-plus yuan in total interest. Cutting the markup before signing is easier and more worthwhile than cutting the house price.
- Payoff: A mortgage isn't "price ÷ years," it's "principal + 30 years of compounding interest." Take 1 million yuan, 30-year equal-installment: at 3.5%, the monthly payment is about 4,490 yuan and total interest about 617,000 yuan; at 4.5%, monthly about 5,069 yuan and total interest about 825,000 yuan—just a 1-percentage-point difference means about 210,000 yuan more in total interest over 30 years, nearly 20% extra. Since China's LPR reform in August 2019, the mortgage rate = the >5-year LPR (cut to 3.5% on 2025-05-20) + the bank's markup, where the markup is set by your profile, city, and bank policy, with a range of several tens of BP. For the same home, Bank A with 0 markup and Bank B with 30 BP (0.3 percentage points) again differ by tens of thousands of yuan in 30-year interest. So "comparison + markup negotiation" is the highest cost-performance move before buying, and it mainly happens before signing—once closed, you can only wait for the repricing date.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: People's Bank of China (2019). Announcement on Reforming and Improving the Loan Prime Rate (LPR) Formation Mechanism (2019 No. 15). <https://www.pbc.gov.cn/zhengcehuobisi/125207/125213/125440/3876551/3877436/index.html>; Loan Prime Rate (LPR) announcement (2025-05-20) authorized by the People's Bank of China via the National Interbank Funding Center. <https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/bumen/202505/content_7024431.htm>
- Note: Rates float with LPR repricing (usually Jan 1 each year or the loan origination date); a falling-rate cycle favors borrowers; this entry focuses on the controllable variable "markup at signing," and the evidence is mechanistic fact (A).

### 2. When the rent-to-price ratio is below the mortgage rate, renting may be more cost-effective
<!-- tags: money=0 time=little will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Drop the fixation that "buying is always right."
- In plain terms: Buying isn't naturally correct. Rent-to-price 2%, loan rate 4%—if you rent and invest the home-buying money, as long as the annualized return exceeds 2% you don't lose. Don't let the "no home, no stability" emotion make the decision for you.
- Payoff: Rent-to-price ratio = annual rent ÷ house price. Data from China Index Academy shows that in December 2024, the average rent-to-price ratio across 50 key cities was only 2.12% (i.e., a static rental yield of 2.12%), with Xiamen, Shenzhen, and Beijing below 1.5%, Guangzhou and Shanghai around 2%, and only Chongqing, Changsha, etc. near 3%, while Harbin/Changchun/Guiyang (3.23%) exceeded 3%. Meanwhile, first-home mortgage rates were generally in the 3.0%–3.5% range. That is, in most core cities, the "cost of owning" (mortgage rate) significantly exceeds the "cost of renting" (rent-to-price ratio), and the spread invested to beat the gap is pure gain. The PBOC's "China Monetary Policy Report, Q2 2024" also notes that on a static rent-to-price basis, rental-housing yield is expected to rise above 3%—in other words, in most cities today, buying to collect rent can't beat the loan interest, so calculate before deciding.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: China Index Academy (2025). December 2024 rent-to-price ratio of residential housing in 50 key cities was 2.12%. <https://www.cih-index.com/news/2025-01-06/51934161.html>; People's Bank of China (2024). China Monetary Policy Report, Q2 2024 (column "Supporting the Sustainable Development of the Rental Housing Industry"). <https://www.cih-index.com/news/2024-12-19/51801279.html>
- Note: The rent-to-price ratio varies enormously by city and location (core vs. far suburb in the same city can differ several-fold), and doesn't deduct vacancy, maintenance, and taxes; the trend judgment (C-grade component) needs calibration with the latest local data—see Entry 11.

### 3. Early repayment saves certain interest
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=family -->
- Cost: A lump of idle cash you can deploy (with no higher-certainty use in the short term).
- In plain terms: Mortgage rate 4–5%, and you can't find a steady investment beating that—early repayment is a steady win. Don't take repayment money to gamble on funds; that's trading certainty for uncertainty.
- Payoff: The only hard criterion for whether to repay early is whether your "steady-investment annualized return" can stably exceed the "mortgage rate." As cited by the Economic Daily from industry estimates: in 2024, outstanding mortgage rates were cut in bulk to LPR−30 BP (about 3.3%), while mortgages issued before 2022 were generally above 5%, a spread of 1.7–2.5 percentage points—precisely the main cause of that year's widespread early repayment. When you have no channel steadily beating the mortgage rate, early repayment "locks in" a risk-free return equal to the rate. Also, equal-installment loans have high early interest share, so the earlier you repay the more you save; most banks waive the prepayment penalty after 1 year of repayment, while within 1 year they may charge 1%–3% (per contract)—calculate whether the penalty or the interest saved is larger before acting. Note that after 2024, many banks introduced appointment queues and capped repayment, so confirm with your lending bank in advance.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Xinhua News / Economic Daily (2025). Is early mortgage repayment worthwhile? <https://www.news.cn/fortune/20250508/7f30da23b5534987a1a3ac66834c5d75/c.html>; Sogou Lawyer (2024). Is early mortgage repayment worthwhile in 2024 (penalty and repayment stage). <https://lvshi.sogou.com/article/detail/DSLTI43WMUL8.html>
- Note: "Steady-investment return" means predictable returns from deposits, government bonds, pure bonds, etc., excluding the volatile part of stocks/funds; if the mortgage is a low-interest housing-fund loan (see Entry 4) or enjoys a discount, there's usually no rush to repay.

### 4. The low-interest housing fund is a benefit—use it up first
<!-- tags: money=0 time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=family -->
- Cost: One extra step of paperwork at loan origination, cooperating with the contribution and withdrawal process.
- In plain terms: The housing-fund rate is a notch below commercial loans; borrow the full quota if you can, don't use pure commercial out of laziness. It's a low-interest red packet from the state—take it or lose it.
- Payoff: The People's Bank of China's 2025-05-07 notice cut individual housing-fund loan rates by 0.25 percentage points from May 8: for over 5 years, first-home from 2.85% to 2.6%, second-home no lower than 3.075%; for 5 years and under (including 5), first-home 2.1%, second-home no lower than 2.525%. Against the same-period commercial first-home rate of about 3.0%–3.5%, on 1 million yuan over 30 years equal-installment, the housing-fund first-home (2.6%) monthly is about 4,003 yuan and total interest about 441,000 yuan, versus the commercial first-home (about 3.05%) saving about 240 yuan a month and about 86,000 yuan in total interest; second-home also saves about 15,000 yuan. Borrow the full housing-fund quota first, fill the shortfall with a commercial loan as a combo loan, and use idle account balances to offset the loan or make annual lump-sum repayments—better than letting it sit and depreciate.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: People's Bank of China (2025). Notice on Lowering Individual Housing-Fund Loan Rates (2025-05-07). <https://big5.www.gov.cn/gate/big5/www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202505/content_7022708.htm>; Lu'an Municipal People's Government (2025). Housing-fund rate cut saves more, pays less! (1-million-30-year interest-savings estimate). <https://www.luan.gov.cn/bmrl/10670333.html>
- Note: Housing-fund loans have contribution-tenure, quota-cap, and cross-city-use restrictions that vary by locality; the low rate itself means "no rush to repay early" (see Entry 3).

### 5. Property is extremely illiquid; you can't sell when you urgently need cash
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=family -->
- Cost: The awareness and discipline to keep enough non-property liquid assets.
- In plain terms: A home is "paper wealth"; it can't save you when you really need cash. Keep a separate emergency fund and freely-withdrawable investments; don't lock the whole family's net worth in one slab of concrete.
- Payoff: From listing to closing, property often takes months, affected by market conditions, school-district policy, floor and orientation, neighbors, even listing volume—far from stocks and funds that can be liquidated the same day. According to 5i5j (Wo Ai Wo Jia) 2024 annual report estimates, China's existing-home turnover rate (transaction volume ÷ total stock) is only about 1%, versus about 4% in the U.S., a four-fold gap, showing that the degree to which "you can sell when you want" is inherently low in China. In 2024, both prices and rents weakened and second-hand listing volume was high; in a buyer's market, realization is slower and bargaining more passive. When cash is urgently needed, you often can only sell cheap or borrow high-interest bridge loans; a family's emergency fund and liquid assets must never be fully tied to property—as a rule, keep at least 6–12 months of expenses in immediately accessible funds.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 5i5j (Wo Ai Wo Jia) (2025). 2024 Annual Report (domestic existing-home turnover rate about 1%, U.S. about 4%). <https://www.cnfin.com/announ/detail/index.html?id=797557962274&code=000560&dannoun=lcdetail&announ=lc>
- Note: Prime core-location liquidity is relatively better, but "relatively better" is still monthly-scale, not daily; the turnover rate is an institutional estimate (B-grade) and fluctuates with the cycle.

### 6. Leveraged home buying amplifies losses in a downturn
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=family -->
- Cost: Vigilance against "leveraged buying always wins."
- In plain terms: A 30% down payment isn't "only losing 30%." If the price drops 30%, your principal is gone; drop further and you owe the bank. Leveraged buying is a two-way magnifier—don't only remember the upside.
- Payoff: A 30% down payment means you use 300,000 yuan to lever 1,000,000 yuan of assets, about 3.3x leverage. A 10% price rise (+100,000) gives your principal a 33% return; but a 30% price drop (−300,000) zeroes your 300,000 principal, entering "negative equity"; if you're then forced to sell, you also bear agent fees and taxes, losing far more than your original outlay. After 2021, some cities' prices retreated 30% or more from their highs (China Index Academy monitors continuous price adjustment in key cities), and early high-leverage buyers faced the dilemma of "house worth less than the debt, but the monthly payment can't stop." Leverage in a down cycle turns small swings into ruin; in a falling market, low leverage or even all-cash is the amulet.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: China Index Academy (2024). China Housing Rental Market Summary and Outlook 2024 (continuous price adjustment, rent-to-price recovering). <https://www.cih-index.com/news/2024-12-19/51801279.html>; CCTV.com (2024). Multiple financial-support policies for real estate landing (outstanding mortgages and price backdrop). <https://news.cctv.com/2024/10/01/ARTIaj74omNQAv9tZJ9INpVk241001.shtml>
- Note: Leverage amplification is a mathematical certainty (strong certainty), but "specific city declines" are historical ranges (B-grade) and are not a prediction of future moves.

### 7. Business/consumer loans illegally flowing into real estate is a time bomb
<!-- tags: money=little time=some will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=family -->
- Cost: Restraint against the lure of "low-interest bridge-to-buy-home."
- In plain terms: Someone teaches you to replace your mortgage with a low-interest business loan—sounds like interest savings, but it's a violation. Once the bank detects and calls the loan, you must repay in full immediately; ordinary people can't bear it. Don't touch it.
- Payoff: Business and consumer loans' contractual purpose is explicitly limited to business or consumption, and may not be used to buy property, settle house payments, or repay a mortgage. The CBIRC, the Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development, and the PBOC jointly issued a notice to prevent business loans from illegally flowing into real estate, requiring banks to strengthen穿透 review of borrower qualifications and loan purposes. Jiangsu CBIRC clearly warns of four risks of "loan-switching-to-lower-rate": one, illegality—using funds contrary to the agreed purpose breaches the contract, the loan may be recalled early with penalty interest, and serious cases carry criminal liability; two, capital-chain break—business loans are often shorter than mortgages, renewal rates/terms can change, and without stable repayment sources you blow up and damage your credit; three, high intermediary "bridge" costs may exceed the original mortgage; four, personal-information leakage. Chongqing CBIRC also warns that illegal use faces early repayment, credit-line cuts, and credit-record inclusion. The little interest saved is far outweighed by the ruin risk of a recalled loan.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of the CBIRC, General Office of the Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development, General Office of the People's Bank of China (2021). Notice on Preventing Business-Purpose Loans from Illegally Flowing into Real Estate. <https://big5.nfra.gov.cn/cn/view/pages/ItemDetail.html?docId=973455>; Jiangsu Regulatory Bureau of the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) (2023). Consumer risk alert: "Loan-switching-to-lower-rate" runs deep, hidden risks to watch. <https://www.nfra.gov.cn/branch/jiangsu/view/pages/common/ItemDetail.html?docId=1099698&itemId=1239>; Chongqing Regulatory Bureau of the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) (2021). Risk alert on business-purpose loans flowing into real estate (2021 No. 9). <https://big5.nfra.gov.cn/branch/chongqing/view/pages/common/ItemDetail.html?docId=1009522&itemId=1985>
- Note: Regulatory provisions are hard constraints (A); individual intermediaries claiming "it can be done" are fraud, and the risk is borne entirely by the borrower.

### 8. Keep the monthly payment under 30% of family income; leave a buffer
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=family -->
- Cost: Restrain the desire to "borrow to the limit for a big house."
- In plain terms: The bank is willing to lend you 50% of income or more in monthly payment, but that's its risk, not yours. Keep the payment within 30% and you won't panic even if unemployed for half a year. Don't use leverage to the limit.
- Payoff: Too high a payment-to-income ratio means that on unemployment, pay cut, or illness you default—at best a credit record hit, at worst foreclosure and principal loss (see Entry 6). At the regulatory level, Jiangxi CBIRC's "Notice on Further Strengthening Personal Loan Risk Management" requires mortgage business to "strictly review down-payment sources" and "ensure the debt-service-to-income ratio is controlled within a reasonable range," showing banks themselves watch income coverage. The internationally used "28/36 rule" suggests: housing payment no more than 28% of pre-tax income, total debt payment no more than 36%. Combined with China's income volatility and social-security coverage, as a rule keep the mortgage payment within one-third of total family income and reserve two-thirds for living and savings, greatly strengthening shock resistance. Note "total family income" should be net of personal tax, social security, and necessary rigid expenses.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Jiangxi Regulatory Bureau of the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA). Notice on Further Strengthening Personal Loan Risk Management (debt-service-to-income control, down-payment source review). <https://www.nfra.gov.cn/branch/jiangxi/view/pages/common/ItemDetail.html?docId=943038&itemId=1513>
- Note: "30%" is a rule of thumb (C-grade component); the specific ratio varies by city income elasticity and job stability; the regulatory file provides a "reasonable range" principle rather than a fixed number.

### 9. Second-hand transaction tax friction is high—don't ignore it
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Count taxes and agent fees into the buy/sell equation.
- In plain terms: Between "buy price" and "sell price" sits tax and the agent; short-term flipping, this friction eats your profit. If you must trade, hold longer to dilute the friction.
- Payoff: A November 2024 tri-ministry announcement (2024 No. 16) substantially optimized transaction taxes: on deed tax, for a family's only and second home with area ≤140㎡ a uniform 1% applies, >140㎡ 1.5%/2% respectively, and second-home in Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Shenzhen also applies—e.g., a 5-million-yuan second home formerly 3% deed tax (150,000 yuan) becomes 1% (50,000 yuan) under the new policy, saving 100,000 yuan; on VAT, individual sales of owned housing held 2+ years (including 2) are exempt across the board (the former differential levy on non-ordinary housing in the four first-tier cities is abolished); on individual income tax, "five-years-and-only" (owned 5+ years and the family's only home) is exempt, otherwise 20% on the differential or an assessed 1%; plus agent fees usually 1%–3% and transfer miscellaneous fees. Even after the new policy, a second-hand home that is under-2-years or not "five-years-and-only" still carries transaction friction of 5%–10% of the price; short-term trading is basically eaten by friction, and the longer you hold, the more the cost is diluted—better value.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration, Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development (2024). Announcement on Policies Concerning Taxation to Promote the Stable and Healthy Development of the Real Estate Market (2024 No. 16). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202411/content_6986876.htm>; Shanghai Tax Bureau (2026). Comprehensive guide to Shanghai individual residential property transaction taxes and fees (VAT/deed-tax/individual-income-tax rules and announcement numbers). <https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/nw17239/20260423/db84274a588149309032977a3df7f916.html>
- Note: Deed tax 1%–3%, VAT exempt over 2 years, individual income tax exempt for "five-years-and-only" are current rules (A); specific rates and exemption recognition follow the local tax authority at transaction time.

### 10. School-district housing premiums carry policy risk
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=family -->
- Cost: The premium paid for the "school seat."
- In plain terms: Spending millions extra for a school-district home bets that policy won't change. But policy is moving toward "multi-school zoning"; once the seat isn't locked, the premium vanishes. Don't pay a sky-high price for uncertainty.
- Payoff: The Ministry of Education made "multi-school zoning" explicit as early as 2014—hot schools are dispersed across multiple zones, and when applications exceed the plan, random assignment is used; the 2022 "Notice on Further Doing Well the Enrollment of Ordinary Primary and Secondary Schools" (Jiao Ji Ting [2022] No. 1) requires regions with unbalanced educational resources to "actively and steadily advance multi-school zoning," and comprehensively promotes "public-private simultaneous enrollment" and balanced class assignment. This means the certainty of "one home locks a famous school" is continuously weakened: zones can be adjusted, enrollment relies on assignment, and high-premium school-district homes may devalue overnight on policy change. Paying a premium far above same-area non-school-district homes to bet on the seat is essentially wagering that policy won't expand capacity, redraw zones, or randomize—and the policy direction is the opposite. Make "school-seat premium goes to zero" a stress-test scenario in your purchase decision.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Education (2014). Implementation Opinions on Further Doing Well the Nearby Exam-Free Promotion from Primary to Junior High (Jiao Ji Yi [2014] No. 1, multi-school zoning). <https://big5.www.gov.cn/gate/big5/www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2014/content_2679352.htm>; General Office of the Ministry of Education (2022). Notice on Further Doing Well the Enrollment of Ordinary Primary and Secondary Schools (Jiao Ji Ting [2022] No. 1). <https://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A06/s3321/202204/t20220401_612689.html?xxgkhide=1>
- Note: The pace of policy implementation varies by city; "premium to zero" is a scenario assumption (C-grade component); the documents themselves are currently effective (A).

### 11. Deduct vacancy and maintenance from rental income—don't count gross rent
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Deduct the hidden costs when bookkeeping.
- In plain terms: The agent says "3% rental yield"—that's gross. Deduct two vacant months, plumbing repairs, property fees, taxes, and net may be just over 1%, about like a bank deposit but with more hassle.
- Payoff: True rental return = (annual rent − vacancy loss − repairs − property fees − management fees − taxes) ÷ house price. China Index Academy's December 2024 average rent-to-price ratio (gross basis) across 50 cities was only 2.12%, and the PBOC also notes that on a static rent-to-price basis the yield is expected to rise above 3%—note this is "gross rent ÷ price" and requires long-run rent growth to reach 3%. In actual holding: vacancy periods (average leasing/rerouting gaps of often 1–2 months, equivalent to 8%–17% annualized rent loss), repair and upkeep, property fees, rental taxes and depreciation stack up, so the net return is often several discounts below the gross, and in many cities net return is only just over 1%—no better than deposits or government bonds, plus the landlord's time and dispute costs. Always use "net rent-to-price ratio" for investment return.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: China Index Academy (2025). December 2024 rent-to-price ratio of residential housing in 50 key cities was 2.12%. <https://www.cih-index.com/news/2025-01-06/51934161.html>; People's Bank of China (2024). China Monetary Policy Report, Q2 2024 (rental-housing yield column). <https://www.cih-index.com/news/2024-12-19/51801279.html>
- Note: 2.12% is the gross rent-to-price ratio (B-grade institutional data); "net return several discounts lower" is an experiential estimate based on vacancy/maintenance (C-grade), varying by property and city.

### 12. Multiple debts and borrowing-to-repay-debt is a prelude to bankruptcy
<!-- tags: money=0 time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=family -->
- Cost: The self-discipline to face the debt and stop borrowing.
- In plain terms: Borrowing to repay debt isn't a solution, it's acceleration before the cliff. Once you rely on new debt for old, stop immediately and take stock—don't borrow more. This is the family-finance red alert.
- Payoff: Credit cards, online loans, and consumer loans plugging each other's holes, with interest compounding into a snowball, eventually leave income unable to cover the minimum payment. The Beijing Regulatory Bureau of the NFRA's risk alert explicitly states "do not choose 'borrowing-to-repay-debt' or 'multiple borrowing,' to avoid debt piling up from inability to repay"; the CBIRC consumer-protection bureau also warns that "over-borrowing often leads to capital cutoff, ultimately trapping the consumer and even the family in difficulty," strictly prohibiting borrowing-to-repay-debt and multiple borrowing. Once "borrowing new to repay old" appears, you must stop the loss immediately: list all debts (principal, rate, due date), cut non-essential spending, and negotiate extension or restructuring with banks, rather than borrowing another sum. This is the red alert of a deteriorating family balance sheet—the earlier you cut, the smaller the loss.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Beijing Regulatory Bureau of the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA). Risk alert: remember the "five dos and five don'ts"—personal loans help a better life (no borrowing-to-repay-debt, no multiple borrowing). <https://www.nfra.gov.cn/branch/beijing/view/pages/common/ItemDetail.html?docId=1070910&itemId=1850>; National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA). Consumer alert: beware over-indebtedness risk, use personal consumer credit loans reasonably and compliantly. <https://www.nfra.gov.cn/cn/view/pages/ItemDetail.html?docId=1033150>
- Note: Regulatory alerts are hard requirements (A); "compounding snowball leads to collapse" is mechanistic common sense, and its appearance should trigger a stop-loss action.
