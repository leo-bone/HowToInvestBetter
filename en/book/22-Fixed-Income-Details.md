# XXII. Fixed Income Details

Fixed income does not mean "fixed making money." Its true meaning is: cash flow and maturity arrangements are more "fixed" than stocks, but prices still fluctuate, issuers can still default, and interest-rate moves can still give you paper losses. This chapter doesn't treat fixed income as one product category, but unpacks it layer by layer "from low to high risk, from transparent to complex," helping you figure out exactly what risk each yuan bears and what compensation it earns.

One core mantra: look at risk first, then return. For any fixed-income type, ask three questions first—to whom is the money lent (credit), locked how long (duration / liquidity), is principal protected (legal status). Answer these three clearly and the "safety cushion" is truly safe.

Be especially wary of two illusions: one is treating wealth products, money market funds, and bond funds as "deposit substitutes" (the November 2022 bond-market swing taught many holders a lesson); the other is being dazzled by the extra interest while missing the default risk it corresponds to. Entries are classified by theme below for you to jump-read by your own needs.

Entries in this section are grouped by theme below (entry numbers in parentheses): I. Risk-free / near-risk-free "return anchor" and principal-protection boundary—treasury tax exemption and risk-free rate (Entry 1), 500,000-yuan deposit insurance cap (Entry 2), money market fund is not a deposit and not principal-protected (Entry 3), after-tax difference between local-government and treasury bonds (Entry 10); II. Fixed income whose net value fluctuates—bond funds don't steadily profit (Entry 4), perpetual bonds / preferred shares have complex terms (Entry 8), bond repo leverage is an institutional play (Entry 9); III. Risk pricing and sensitivity tools—high-yield bonds' credit spread isn't free (Entry 6), duration measures rate sensitivity (Entry 7), convertible bonds have a bond floor below and no cap above but aren't principal-protected (Entry 5).

### 1. Treasuries almost never default; the rate is the opportunity-cost anchor
<!-- tags: money=0 time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Zero (national credit).
- In plain terms: Treasury interest is also exempt from personal income tax. Its point isn't how much it earns, but giving you a yardstick of "how much you can get without taking risk."
- Payoff: Backed by national credit, treasuries have theoretically extremely low default probability; their yield is treated as the "risk-free rate," your yardstick for judging whether any investment is worth the risk—only when an asset's expected return clearly exceeds the same-maturity treasury does taking extra risk make sense. Take the 10-year treasury: its yield stepped down from about 14% in 1996, the 3%–4% range in 2008, to 2.5% in 2020, 2.7% in 2022, and by end-December 2024 had fallen to around 1.7%, a record low since 2002, even briefly breaking below 2% on December 2, 2024. This means those who bought long-term treasuries in 2024 got a risk-free coupon much lower than a decade earlier, but it conversely highlights a "safe-asset scarcity"—the more money there is, the more one must accept a lower guaranteed return. In addition, interest on treasuries held by individuals is exempt from personal income tax (Article 4(2) of the Individual Income Tax Law), so you take home more at the same nominal rate.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Taxation Administration (2019). 国债利息收入是否免征个人所得税 (Article 4(2) of the Individual Income Tax Law). <https://liaoning.chinatax.gov.cn/art/2019/8/30/art_1991_23923.html>; China Central Depository & Clearing (2025). 2024年利率债市场回顾与2025年展望. <https://www.ccdc.com.cn/Fmi/Thinktank/Article/cb2025/cb202501/cb20250103/202501/t20250126_854519784.html>
- Note: Treasury personal-income-tax exemption applies to bonds issued by the Ministry of Finance that individuals hold; interest on policy financial bonds is also exempt, but only local-government bonds issued after 2012 are tax-exempt (see Entry 10). The risk-free rate is a theoretical anchor; in practice short-term financing bills and money market fund returns are often below same-maturity treasuries due to tax-exemption and liquidity differences. Cross-reference: Entry 3, Entry 10.

### 2. Deposit insurance 500,000-yuan cap: principal + interest within 500,000 per single bank fully protected
<!-- tags: money=0 time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Spread large deposits across different banks.
- In plain terms: If you have a lot of money, don't put it all in one bank. The part above 500,000 theoretically depends on whether that bank fails. Spread across a few and you sleep well.
- Payoff: For the same depositor at the same insured institution, all insured deposit accounts' principal and interest combined within 500,000 yuan are paid in full; the excess is satisfied from the liquidated property of the insured institution according to law. Per the central bank, the 500,000-yuan limit covers over 99.6% of China's depositors' entire deposits. Note three points: first, "same bank" not "same account"—your deposits at different branches of Bank A are combined; second, what's protected is "deposits" (domestic and foreign currency, personal and institutional), not interbank deposits or executives' deposits at their own institution; third, wealth products, funds, and structured deposits are not deposits and are not protected. For ordinary families, spreading idle money above 500,000 across 2–3 different banks is steadier than chasing some high-interest small bank. Deposit insurance is paid by the bank; you pay nothing, and full payment is made within 7 working days after a trigger event.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: People's Bank of China (2024). 存款保险条例修改了保障额度吗 (maximum payout limit is 500,000 yuan). <https://wzdt.pbc.gov.cn/eportal/ui?pageId=77c3557bd521439ea5cd869f5393ba98&msgDataId=d3f5c72a0157407e97b4998676c34ef2>; People's Bank of China Fuzhou Central Sub-branch (2015). 积极有效实施存款保险制度 充分保障存款人合法权益 (payout limit). <https://fuzhou.pbc.gov.cn/fuzhou/126870/4011508/index.html>
- Note: 500,000 is combined for "the same insured institution," not by account or by family; splitting names between spouses can raise the family's protection cap. Wealth products, structured deposits, and treasuries are outside the protection. Grade A because it comes from a State Council regulation and official PBOC interpretation—high authority.

### 3. A money market fund is not a deposit; not principal-protected but very low risk
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Understand it's not principal-protected and has a T+0 redemption cap.
- In plain terms: A money fund isn't a demand deposit, doesn't protect principal, but has rarely lost money historically. It's fine for emergency funds; don't expect it to beat inflation.
- Payoff: Money market funds mainly invest in short-term, high-credit money instruments (interbank certificates of deposit, short-term treasuries, bank deposits, etc.); not principal-protected but rarely lost money historically, a good home for emergency funds—higher return than demand deposits, flexible access. But note two boundaries: first, it's not a deposit and not covered by deposit insurance, and in extreme liquidity events its single-day return can also turn negative (e.g., in the November 2022 bond-market swing money fund returns clearly dropped, with individual products breaking the buck); second, in 2018 the CSRC and PBOC jointly issued the Guiding Opinions on Further Regulating the Internet Sales and Redemption Services of Money Market Funds, setting the single money fund's single sales institution "T+0 redemption cash-out" daily cap at 10,000 yuan, with the excess only via ordinary redemption (T+1 or longer). So don't pile all emergency funds into one money fund, and don't expect it to beat inflation—its positioning is "cash management," not "value growth."
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: China Securities Regulatory Commission, People's Bank of China (2018). 关于进一步规范货币市场基金互联网销售、赎回相关服务的指导意见 (Article 2: T+0 redemption cash-out no higher than 10,000 yuan per day). <https://www.chinanews.com.cn/fortune/2018/06-01/8528372.shtml>; China Net (2018). 央行、证监会：规范货币市场基金互联网赎回服务. <https://news.china.com.cn/2018-06/01/content_51567187.htm>
- Note: Money funds aren't principal-protected; although the November 2022 swing saw no broad losses, returns plunged and individual products broke the buck, proving extremes exist. The T+0 cap applies only to "fast-redemption cash-out"; ordinary redemption isn't limited to 10,000 but arrives slowly. Grade B because it stems from regulatory rules and the industry's generally low-risk character, not zero risk. Cross-reference: Entry 4.

### 4. Bond funds fluctuate; they don't "steadily profit"
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Bear the paper drawdown when rates rise.
- In plain terms: A bond fund isn't a deposit substitute. When rates rise, the bond fund's net value falls. Its "steadiness" is relative, not absolute.
- Payoff: A bond fund's "steadiness" is relative, not absolute. The November 2022 bond-market plunge is the best textbook: on November 14 the ChinaBond Composite Wealth (Total Value) Index fell 0.41% in a single day, its largest single-day drop since 2017; per Wind, by November 17, of 1,752 pure-bond funds in the whole market, only 61 had positive returns over the past week, a loss ratio as high as 96%, average drop about 0.54%, and 665 (38%) hit their largest drawdown since inception; individual funds dropped over 1% in a week, and some convertible-bond funds drew down over 3%. Over the same period bank-wealth-product break-the-buck counts rose rapidly, with several products hitting massive-redemption caps. The causal chain: rates rise → bond fund net value falls → clients redeem → forced bond selling → further price drops. Conclusion: pure-bond funds can have 2%–5% annual volatility, and in a rate-up cycle they lose too—treating them as "deposit substitutes" is a cognitive error.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: China Economic Net (2022). 债市连续下挫 多只债基单日跌近1% (ChinaBond Composite Wealth Index single-day −0.41%). <https://finance.ce.cn/jjpd/jjpdgd/202211/17/t20221117_38234646.shtml>; Securities Times / Broker (2022). 债市跌到哪了？创两年来最大调整 (96% pure-bond funds negative over past week). <https://www.toutiao.com/article/7167252372152910388>
- Note: November 2022 is a single extreme sample; normally pure-bond annual volatility is smaller; but the cognitive-correction value of "bond funds aren't deposits" is high. The longer the duration of a pure-bond fund, the harder it falls—see Entry 7. Grade B: supported by public empirical evidence (Wind, media statistics) but not an academic long-run sample.

### 5. Convertible bonds: bond floor below, no cap above, but not principal-protected
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Understand the two-sidedness of "bond floor + conversion option."
- In plain terms: Convertible bonds sound "offense when up, defense when down," but catching a premium convertible at a high is also a loss. They're steadier than stocks, far wilder than pure bonds.
- Payoff: A convertible bond = "bond + conversion option"; in a bear market the bond floor catches it, in a bull market it converts to stock and follows the rise, hence often marketed as "offense when up, defense when down." But 2022–2023 reality poured cold water: Qilu Convertible debuted and broke issue on December 19, 2022, the only bank convertible to break issue that year; in 2023 Soue Convertible fell to a historic low of 31.666 yuan/unit, and Landun Convertible became the first delisted convertible, with holders facing higher losses under reorganization and liquidation. By end-2022 the whole-market average convertible price was about 127 yuan and average conversion premium about 46%, meaning most convertibles traded far above "bond floor + conversion value," the premium embedding overpricing of the option. Conclusion: convertibles swing less than stocks and far wilder than pure bonds; catching a high-premium convertible at the top (conversion premium over 100%) can also halve you, and it is by no means a "principal-protected" product.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: 21st Century Business Herald (2023). 齐鲁银行拟下修可转债转股价 机构称2023年战略性看多银行转债 (Qilu Convertible breaks issue). <https://m.21jingji.com/article/20230110/herald/fa4be95a86a78df0cc1fa5225141b084_ths.html>; NetEase / Securities Times (2023). 可转债投资价值仍存比较优势 (first delisting, Soue Convertible 31.666 yuan). <https://www.163.com/dy/article/I4IVE71U053469RG.html>
- Note: Convertible risk is highly correlated with its premium rate; at low premium (near the bond floor) downside is limited, at high premium it converges with stocks. The first delisting (Landun, Soue) broke the "must convert, must repay" illusion. Grade B: cases come from regulatory and market reporting—individual risk, not systemic. Cross-reference: Entry 6 (credit), Entry 7 (duration).

### 6. High-yield bonds = high risk; the credit spread isn't free
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Be wary of bonds / bond funds with "noticeably high yields."
- In plain terms: Why is its interest so much higher than treasuries? Because the market fears it won't pay. That extra interest is compensation for you bearing "possible total loss."
- Payoff: Credit spread = a credit bond's yield − same-maturity treasury yield; the extra interest is compensation for "it might default," not a free lunch. Taking overseas high-yield bonds (junk bonds) as reference: per Moody's long-run statistics, the median annual default rate of high-yield bonds is about 3.4%, spiking above 8% in crisis years (2008, 2001–2002, 2015–2016); once defaulted, the historical average recovery rate of senior unsecured bonds is only about 35%–40% (as low as 22.85% during the 2022 inflation shock), meaning per 100 yuan face value you recover only about 35–40 yuan on average, a loss of about 60%. The logic is the same for domestic low-rated credit bonds (e.g., AA, AA−, or even defaulted issuers): the higher the coupon, the higher the default probability the market assigns it. So when you see a bond fund or private bond with "noticeably high yield," first ask "why is it so much higher than treasuries," not "how much more can I earn."
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: New Capital (2025). Managing Defaults in a High Yield Portfolio (citing Moody's data: recovery rate 35.63, default rate median 3.41%). <https://www.newcapital.com/en/singapore/insights/Managing-Defaults-in-a-High-Yield-Portfolio.html>; Hong Kong Economic Journal (2024). 高收益债券为何叫垃圾债券？一文看清回报、风险与投资级别债券分别 (Moody's median default rate 3.4%, recovery rate 35%–40%). <https://www.hkej.com/wm/article/id/4344326>
- Note: The cited Moody's is overseas-market data; domestic credit-bond recovery-rate statistics vary in caliber and are sparsely disclosed, so direct application needs discounting; but the logic "high coupon = high-risk compensation" is universal. Domestic has seen multiple credit-bond defaults with low recovery (some under half face value). Grade B because it's mainly overseas samples + logical deduction.

### 7. Duration: a ruler for measuring rate sensitivity
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Look at the duration metric of bonds / bond funds.
- In plain terms: Duration is "if rates rise 1%, how many points does my bond fall." Check duration before buying a long-bond fund, so you know where you stand.
- Payoff: Duration measures the sensitivity of a bond / bond portfolio's price to interest-rate moves, roughly "if rates move 1%, my bond moves inversely by about how many percentage points." Strictly, modified duration D* satisfies the approximation ΔP/P ≈ −D*×Δy: if the portfolio's modified duration is 5, a 1% rate rise drops price about 5%; duration 12, about 12%. Reality differs greatly—take the ChinaBond 30-year Treasury Wealth Index: its duration is about 18, so a 1 basis point (0.01%) rate move moves the index inversely about 0.18%; on March 11, 2024 the index's yield rose 4 bp and the index fell 0.69%, intuitively showing "the longer the duration, the more rate-sensitive." For retail, check a long-bond fund's duration before buying: the longer the duration, the harder it falls when rates rise / tightening, but also the more it rises when rates fall. Duration is your first ruler for predicting bond-fund volatility.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Morningstar. Duration | Investing Terms and Definitions (modified duration: rate moves 1% price moves about duration %). <https://www.morningstar.com/investing-terms/duration>; East Money / Pengyang Fund (2024). 玩转30年国债ETF——债券波动看久期 (30-year treasury index duration about 18). <https://caifuhao.eastmoney.com/news/20240402162021292243230>
- Note: Modified duration is a linear approximation; large rate moves need "convexity" correction; bonds with embedded options (callable / puttable) need effective duration. Retail buying bond funds just needs to look at the "duration" disclosed in quarterly reports or the portfolio's weighted remaining maturity. Grade B: definition from standard finance methods and index-provider estimates.

### 8. Perpetual bonds / preferred shares: complex terms, default to caution
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Don't touch if you don't know the terms.
- In plain terms: It's called a "bond" but may not repay your principal, and interest can be deferred. Terms are a notch more complex than ordinary bonds; if you don't understand, don't buy.
- Payoff: A perpetual bond (undated capital bond) is nominally a "bond" but has distinct equity features: no fixed maturity, the issuer can autonomously defer current and deferred interest to the next payment date without it being a default; it contains write-down or conversion clauses—upon a trigger event (e.g., core solvency ratio below 30%) it may be directly written down or converted, and the investor has no right to petition the issuer into bankruptcy. Take insurance-version perpetual bonds: their repayment order is before common shares and preferred shares but after ordinary bonds and subordinated bonds, i.e., "subordination within subordination." So its yield is higher than ordinary bonds, corresponding to term uncertainty: interest may be canceled, principal may be written down, and it may never be redeemed. Preferred shares are similar—dividends can be cumulative or non-cumulative, and the company can decide not to pay. The advice for retail is "if you don't understand the terms, don't touch"—such instruments mainly target institutions and qualified investors.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: People's Bank of China, China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission (2022). 关于保险公司发行无固定期限资本债券有关事项的通知 (Articles 7, 10: interest cancellation, write-down / conversion risk). <https://big5.nfra.gov.cn/cn/view/pages/rulesDetail.html?docId=1066561>; People's Daily (2022). 险企永续债9月开闸，保险业再添"补血"新工具 (undated, deferrable interest). <https://www.peopleapp.com/rmharticle/30029808433>
- Note: Bank-version perpetual bonds opened in 2019, insurance-version landed September 2022; mostly an inter-institution market, retail's direct participation channels are limited, often held indirectly via "fixed-income+" products. Grade C because terms are complex, retail data scarce, and default samples very few. Cross-reference: Entry 6 (subordination risk).

### 9. Bond-repo leverage is an institutional play; retail beware
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Give up "repo-amplified returns."
- In plain terms: Institutions use repo to borrow and buy bonds to amplify returns; retail following can get blown up when funding tightens. Ordinary allocation doesn't need it.
- Payoff: Bond repo (pledged repo) is institutions' tool to "pledge securities and borrow" for leverage: the positive-repo party pledges bonds to the reverse-repo party to raise short-term funds, buys more bonds, amplifying coupon returns. But leverage is a double-edged sword—when rates reverse, funding costs rise and pledged-bond valuations fall, compounded by "roll-over" being interrupted, losses amplify rapidly and can even trigger passive selling. Regulation sets clear red lines (Notice on Regulating Bond Market Participants' Bond Trading Business, PBOC [2017] No. 302): deposit-type institutions' self-operated positive/reverse repo balance may not exceed 80% of prior-quarter-end net assets, other financial institutions (brokers, funds, etc.) no more than 120% of prior-month-end net assets, and public funds and other public non-bank products no more than 40% of prior-day net assets (closed-operation and avoidance-strategy funds no more than 100%). Ordinary investors can hardly obtain repo eligibility and can't withstand liquidity shocks, so this "return enhancement" is basically irrelevant to retail—don't blindly imitate.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: People's Bank of China, CBIRC, CSRC, SAFE (2017). 关于规范债券市场参与者债券交易业务的通知 (PBOC [2017] No. 302, Article 9: leverage-ratio red lines). <https://neris.csrc.gov.cn/falvfagui/rdqsHeader/mainbody?navbarId=3&secFutrsLawId=745777672752458084>; Sina Finance (2026). 一文看懂银行间债券回购 (leverage red lines and term caps). <https://finance.sina.cn/2026-09-17/detail-iniscekk9292068.d.html?vt=4>
- Note: Document 302 also requires repo to be booked on-balance-sheet, max term no more than 365 days, and strictly no under-the-table agreements. Retail participating in on-exchange reverse repo (e.g., GC001) is the "lender" side, not leveraging, with very low risk. Grade C because it targets institutional behavior, weakly directly relevant to most readers. Cross-reference: Entry 4 (volatility), Entry 7 (duration).

### 10. The after-tax difference between local-government and treasury bonds
<!-- tags: money=little time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Compare the after-tax returns of same-maturity local-government and treasury bonds.
- In plain terms: Local-government bond interest is also tax-exempt, slightly higher than same-maturity treasuries, at the cost of slightly worse liquidity and slightly weaker credit. Large steady funds can allocate moderately.
- Payoff: Local-government bonds and treasuries are both sovereign / quasi-sovereign credit; the key tax difference is: per the Notice of the Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration on Income Tax Exemption for Local Government Bond Interest (Cai Shui [2013] No. 5), enterprise and individual interest income from local government bonds issued in 2012 and later years is exempt from enterprise and individual income tax—the same as treasury interest exemption (Article 4(2) of the Individual Income Tax Law). Therefore at the same maturity, because local-government bonds have slightly worse liquidity and slightly weaker credit than treasuries, their coupon is typically a few to a dozen-odd BP higher, and after-tax take-home may actually be better. The cost: local-government bond secondary-market trading is less active than treasuries, so the probability of discount realization when you urgently need cash is higher; and fiscal strength varies greatly across provinces—though default is extremely rare, credit divergence objectively exists. Large steady funds can moderately increase allocation, but don't sacrifice liquidity for that bit of premium.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (2013). 关于地方政府债券利息免征所得税问题的通知 (Cai Shui [2013] No. 5, Article 1). <https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c102416/c5204397/content.html>; State Taxation Administration (2019). 国债利息收入是否免征个人所得税 (Article 4(2) of the Individual Income Tax Law). <https://liaoning.chinatax.gov.cn/art/2019/8/30/art_1991_23923.html>
- Note: Tax exemption applies only to "local government bonds" (provincial government issuance); urban-investment bonds and state-owned-enterprise bonds are not included—the latter still pay tax and have different credit risk. The after-tax difference between same-maturity local-government and treasury bonds is usually only a few BP, limited significance for small amounts. Grade C because the difference is small and needs judgment against tax amount. Cross-reference: Entry 1.
