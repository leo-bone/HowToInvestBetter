# VIII. Tax-Advantaged Accounts

Every yuan you legally save on taxes is a yuan earned net. This chapter covers only accounts and tax-advantaged moves that are clearly spelled out in policy and available to everyone—no gray areas. The nature of tax is that it re-slices your money at different stages and at different rates: hold assets at the "low-rate" stage, deploy within "pre-tax-deduction" limits, and the snowball of long-term compounding gets cut away far less. For ordinary people, the first principle of tax optimization isn't hunting for loopholes—it's using the concessions the state explicitly gives you to the full. They're usually zero-cost and low-risk; they just require you to "know and act."

One boundary needs to be stated up front: every conclusion in this chapter is based on China's current tax law and signed international tax treaties, and takes the "resident individual" as the main perspective. On matters like marriage, inheritance, and cross-border status, cases differ greatly; for entries flagged 〔disputed〕, defer to a professional lawyer or the competent tax authority—don't copy online templates. Tax policy also shifts year to year (e.g., special additional deduction thresholds, the private pension pilot scope), so make it a habit to check once before each annual reconciliation (settlement).

The entries below are grouped by theme (entry numbers in parentheses): tax-deferred pension accounts (Entries 1, 5, 9, 10); stocks and capital gains (Entries 2, 3); interest and various pre-tax deductions (Entries 6, 7, 8); family asset isolation and inheritance (Entries 4, 11, 12); cross-border tax-information exchange (Entry 13).

### 1. Private pension account: tax deferral
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Opening an account (a unique private pension fund account opened through a commercial bank) plus planning for the annual quota of at most 12,000 yuan, and generally you can't freely withdraw before retirement.
- In plain terms: The higher your income, the more valuable the pre-tax deduction. This 12,000 yuan is a "tax-free investment quota" the policy gives you; not using it is the same as paying tax for nothing.
- Payoff: The private pension account applies tax deferral across three stages—contribution, investment, and withdrawal. At contribution, up to 12,000 yuan per year is deducted in full from your comprehensive income or business income (Ministry of Finance and STA Announcement 2022 No. 34, Article 1); at the investment stage, earnings inside the account are not taxed for the time being; at withdrawal, the amount is not folded into comprehensive income but taxed separately at a 3% rate. For taxpayers in the 10%, 20%, or even higher marginal brackets, it's effectively moving principal taxed at a high rate to be taken out at a 3% rate. Rough estimate for someone in the 20% marginal bracket contributing 12,000 yuan/year, invested 30 years at 6% annualized: the account's principal plus interest is about 687,000 yuan after 30 years; without the tax break, the same principal under 20% taxation would pay hundreds of thousands more in tax, while the 3% withdrawal tax is only about 20,000 yuan—a substantial net saving. Nationwide rollout began in 2024 (Announcement 2024 No. 21).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (2022). Announcement on Individual Income Tax Policy Concerning Private Pension (Ministry of Finance & STA Announcement 2022 No. 34, Article 1). <https://szs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202211/t20221104_3849838.htm>; General Office of the State Council (2022). Opinions on Promoting the Development of Private Pensions (Guo Ban Fa [2022] No. 7). <https://www.ndrc.gov.cn/fggz/jyysr/jysrsbxf/202205/t20220519_1324824.html>; Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (2024). Announcement on Implementing Private Pension Individual Income Tax Preferential Policy Nationwide (Ministry of Finance & STA Announcement 2024 No. 21). <https://shaanxi.chinatax.gov.cn/art/2024/12/20/art_33494_629720.html>
- Note: Applies only to workers participating in basic pension insurance within China; withdrawal conditions include reaching retirement age, complete loss of working capacity, or settling abroad; the contribution deduction certificate is issued by the private pension information management service platform; early withdrawal is subject not only to the 3% tax but also to account-rule restrictions.

### 2. Differential dividend tax: tax-free if held long term
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Holding stocks/funds for a full year (not selling), and they must be stocks of a listed company acquired on a "public issuance and transfer market."
- In plain terms: Reinvesting dividends plus "holding a full year" makes the dividend end nearly tax-free. Flipping dividend stocks short-term gets you taxed instead—not worth it.
- Payoff: Dividends from stocks held over one year are exempt from individual income tax; holdings of one month or less (inclusive) are counted in full and taxed at 20%; holdings over one month up to one year (inclusive) are counted at 50% and uniformly taxed at 20% (Caishui [2015] No. 101, Article 1). Example: 100,000 yuan principal at a 3% annual dividend yield = 3,000 yuan in annual dividends—sell in under a month and you pay 20% = 600 yuan; hold a full year and you pay 0. Long-term holdings of quality dividend stocks make the dividend end nearly tax-free, and combined with reinvested compounding it's the easiest after-tax boost for ordinary people. The "holding period" is calculated continuously on a first-in-first-out basis, from purchase to the day before sale.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration, CSRC (2015). Notice on the Differentiated Individual Income Tax Policy for Listed-Company Dividends (Caishui [2015] No. 101, Article 1). <https://www.chinatax.gov.cn/chinatax/n810341/n810765/n1465977/201510/c1967339/content.html>; State Taxation Administration 12366 (2015). How Individual Dividends from Listed Companies Are Taxed. <https://12366.chinatax.gov.cn/sscx/detail?code=0000000002013050300023>
- Note: Applies to SSE/SZSE listed stocks and NEEQ-listed companies; dividends passed through to individuals via limited partnerships or contractual funds may not qualify for the differential treatment; the record date must be after September 8, 2015 for this notice to apply.

### 3. Capital gains: A-shares temporarily exempt—but don't trade just for that
<!-- tags: money=0 time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: A bit of knowledge about the tax system, plus the discipline to curb frequent trading.
- In plain terms: A-share capital gains being tax-free is the current state, but don't make meaningless frequent trades just to "avoid tax"—friction and timing losses far outweigh the tax. Tax isn't the main reason you lose money; itchy hands are.
- Payoff: Gains from individuals transferring SSE/SZSE listed stocks have remained temporarily exempt from individual income tax since January 1, 1997 (Caishuizi [1998] No. 61); the buy-sell spread on publicly offered fund units is also in the tax-exempt scope. Compare the U.S.: short-term capital gains are taxed at ordinary income rates (up to 37%), while long-term (held over one year) capital gains fall in three brackets of 0%/15%/20% (IRS Topic 409). A-share price-difference tax exemption is a real institutional dividend, but trading still incurs securities transaction stamp duty (currently 0.05% on the seller side only, halved since August 28, 2023) and broker commissions—the frictional cost of frequent trading far exceeds the capital-gains tax that wasn't levied in the first place.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (1998). Notice on Continued Temporary Exemption of Individual Income Tax on Gains from Transferring Stocks (Caishuizi [1998] No. 61). <https://shanghai.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfw/zcfgk/grsds/200402/t288650.html>; Internal Revenue Service (2025). Topic no. 409, Capital gains and losses. <https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409>
- Note: Only gains from transferring "listed-company stocks" are temporarily exempt; restricted-share transfers have been taxed at 20% since 2009 (Caishui [2009] No. 167); overseas-market stocks follow the host country's tax law, and U.S. long-term capital gains may still hit the additional 3.8% net investment income tax.

### 4. Family asset isolation and pre-marital property
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=family -->
- Cost: A bit of legal and account planning (separate accounts, transfer notes, written agreements), and consulting a lawyer when needed.
- In plain terms: Keep investment accounts and liability accounts as separate as possible, and leave a paper trail for large assets (transfer notes, agreements). This isn't scheming—it's putting a firewall around the family's assets.
- Payoff: Article 1063 of the Civil Code clearly states that one party's pre-marital property is that party's separate property; Article 1065 allows both spouses to agree in writing that income during marriage and pre-marital property belong separately, jointly, or partly separately to each, legally binding on both (to assert against a third party, the counterparty must have knowledge). Through separate accounts, transfer notes, and evidence of gifts/pre-marital agreements, you lower the risk that marital breakup or personal debt spills over and pierces your investment principal. Example: stocks or funds bought with a personal card before marriage and held long term—if that account is isolated from the post-marriage salary card and the source of funds is clear, they're easier to define as separate property in a divorce.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code of the People's Republic of China (Articles 1063, 1065). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/ssmfdyflvdtpgz/202008/t20200831_478417.shtml>; Department of Justice of Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region (2022). Compilation and interpretation of Civil Code "couple property" provisions. <https://sft.xinjiang.gov.cn/xjsft/fzsj/202209/9abcedfd9fab4b1cb36820233a7e6ebe.shtml>
- Note: 〔disputed〕specific arrangements involve emotions and individual cases—consult a professional lawyer rather than copying online templates; post-marriage "income from production, business, or investment" may be deemed community property, and the isolation effect requires proof tied to fund flows—signing an agreement doesn't settle everything.

### 5. Private pension withdrawal taxed at 3%
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Understanding the "deferred contribution, 3% at withdrawal" rule, opening the unique fund account, and keeping certificates until retirement.
- In plain terms: The private pension isn't "fully tax-free"—it's "pay later, pay less." At withdrawal you pay only 3%, lower than most people's marginal rate; the higher your income, the more it pays off.
- Payoff: The private pension isn't fully tax-free but "deferred payment, low-rate payment." The contribution and investment stages defer individual income tax, and at withdrawal you pay 3% separately, not folded into comprehensive income (2022 No. 34 Article 1; 2024 No. 21 nationwide). It pays off for anyone in the 10% or higher comprehensive-income marginal bracket: at a 20% marginal rate, every 12,000 yuan contributed saves 2,000 yuan in tax upfront and pays only 360 yuan at withdrawal—a net saving of about 1,640 yuan/year, still substantial after 30-year discounting; even low-income earners exactly at the 3% marginal rate gain slightly from tax-free compounding during the investment period, just a smaller saving.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (2022). Announcement on Individual Income Tax Policy Concerning Private Pension (Ministry of Finance & STA Announcement 2022 No. 34, Article 1). <https://szs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202211/t20221104_3849838.htm>; Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (2024). Announcement on Implementing Private Pension Individual Income Tax Preferential Policy Nationwide (Ministry of Finance & STA Announcement 2024 No. 21). <https://shaanxi.chinatax.gov.cn/art/2024/12/20/art_33494_629720.html>
- Note: 2024 No. 21 expanded the pilot nationwide; withdrawal is withheld by the commercial bank that opened the fund account; you can withdraw only upon reaching basic-pension age and other conditions, the method once chosen cannot be changed, and upon death the account assets are inheritable.

### 6. Treasury and local-government bond interest is tax-free
<!-- tags: money=little time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Zero. You only need to prioritize identifying "treasury bonds / local government bonds" rather than taxable fixed income when allocating steady funds.
- In plain terms: Same 3% interest—deposit interest is temporarily exempt but the logic holds, and treasury-bond interest isn't taxed a cent. For large steady funds, the treasury bond's "after-tax advantage" is real money.
- Payoff: Article 4(2) of the Individual Income Tax Law exempts interest on treasury bonds and state-issued financial bonds from individual income tax; Article 3 generally taxes interest, dividends, and bonuses at a 20% proportional rate. At the same 3% nominal yield: 1,000,000 yuan in a 3% tax-free treasury bond yields 30,000 yuan/year kept in full; switch to an ordinary interest-bearing asset taxed at 20% and you net only 24,000 yuan—an after-tax gap of 6,000 yuan/year. Local government bond interest is likewise exempt and often carries a higher coupon than same-term treasury bonds; for high-tax-rate, stability-seeking large funds, the after-tax advantage grows with principal.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). Individual Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China (Articles 4, 3). <https://sichuan.chinatax.gov.cn/art/2018/10/30/art_2683_100535.html>
- Note: Only "state-issued financial bonds" are exempt, with scope per the Ministry of Finance's issuance criteria; interest on corporate bonds, private placement bonds, and non-standard fixed income is still taxed at 20%—don't conflate them when allocating.

### 7. Tax-preference health insurance: pre-tax deduction quota
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Within the annual pre-tax deduction quota of at most 2,400 yuan, allocate to designated health insurance and confirm the policy carries a "tax-preference identification code."
- In plain terms: A 2,400 yuan annual quota—buying designated health insurance offsets tax. The quota is small, but you get "protection + tax saving" together; it's a no-loss add-on.
- Payoff: Individuals who buy qualifying commercial health insurance can deduct the expense before calculating taxable income, capped at 2,400 yuan/year (200 yuan/month), an extra deduction beyond the standard expense threshold of the Individual Income Tax Law (Caishui [2017] No. 39, Article 1). At a 10% marginal rate you save 240 yuan/year, at 20% save 480 yuan, at 45% save 1,080 yuan—small quota but "protection + tax saving" combined. From 2023 the products expanded to medical insurance, long-term care insurance, and critical-illness insurance (Jingu i [2023] No. 2); the insured is not limited to oneself and can be spouse, children, or parents.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration, China Insurance Regulatory Commission (2017). Notice on Extending the Pilot Individual Income Tax Policy for Commercial Health Insurance Nationwide (Caishui [2017] No. 39, Article 1). <https://12366.chinatax.gov.cn/fwtz/print?bh=0000000002015072800012&iszs=1>; Shanghai Municipal Tax Service, STA (2017). Notice on Extending the Pilot Individual Income Tax Policy for Commercial Health Insurance Nationwide (Caishui [2017] No. 39, full text). <https://shanghai.chinatax.gov.cn/gate/big5/shanghai.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfw/zcfgk/grsds/201705/t432115.html>
- Note: The policy must carry a "tax-preference identification code" (STA Announcement 2017 No. 17)—no code, no deduction; unit-purchased policies are treated as individual purchases within the limit; premiums over 2,400 yuan in a year aren't deductible; on surrender you must promptly inform the withholding unit.

### 8. Special additional deductions: deductions everyone can use
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Fill in child education, mortgage interest, elderly support, etc. in the individual income tax app, and check once before each annual reconciliation.
- In plain terms: These deductions cost you no extra money—just fill out a form. Don't fill it and you pay tax for nothing. Check it every year before reconciliation.
- Payoff: The standard (Guofa [2018] No. 41): child education 1,000 yuan/month per child (parents may split 500 each); continuing-education degree 400 yuan/month (max 48 months) or 3,600 yuan in the year of certification; serious-illness medical self-paid portion over 15,000 yuan deducted as actual within an 80,000 yuan cap; first-home mortgage interest 1,000 yuan/month (max 240 months); housing rent 800/1,100/1,500 yuan/month by city tier; elderly support 2,000 yuan/month for only children, non-only children share capped at 1,000 yuan/month each. For a dual-income household + 1 child + mortgage + elderly support, annual deductions can reach (1,000+1,000+2,000)×12 = 48,000 yuan; at a 20% marginal rate that's 9,600 yuan/year saved—pure tax reduction at zero cost.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council (2018). Interim Measures for Special Additional Deductions for Individual Income Tax (Guofa [2018] No. 41). <https://www.gov.cn/xinwen/2018-12/22/content_5351198.htm>; Chinese Government Website (2018). The Measures clarify six special additional deductions (interpretation). <https://www.gov.cn/xinwen/2018-12/24/content_5351641.htm>
- Note: Standards took effect January 1, 2019 and may later adjust with livelihood spending; housing rent and first-home mortgage interest can't be claimed together; the information is shared and verified by education, medical-insurance, and housing departments, and false filings carry credit and legal risks.

### 9. Enterprise / occupational annuity: don't miss the second pillar
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Confirm whether your employer has set up an annuity and your personal contribution ratio, and learn the transfer-and-continuation rules when you leave.
- In plain terms: Don't overlook an employer annuity—it's "forced saving + tax preference" on top of your salary; when you leave, understand the transfer-and-continuation rules.
- Payoff: If the employer sets up an annuity, the employer's contribution into your personal account isn't taxed at the time; your personal contribution within 4% of your contribution-wage tax base is deducted from current taxable income; and earnings distributed to your personal account from fund investment operations aren't taxed for the time being (Caishui [2013] No. 103, Articles 1–2). This is "forced saving + tax deferral" beyond your salary, with sizable long-run roll-up. Example: monthly salary 20,000 yuan, annual contribution-wage base 240,000 yuan, personal 4% = 9,600 yuan deductible pre-tax, with employer matching also deferred; at 5% annualized over 20 years the account can reach several hundred thousand yuan, growing tax-free throughout.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security, State Taxation Administration (2013). Notice on Individual Income Tax Issues Concerning Enterprise and Occupational Annuities (Caishui [2013] No. 103, Articles 1–2). <https://shanghai.chinatax.gov.cn/gate/big5/shanghai.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfw/zcfgk/grsds/201312/t405472.html>
- Note: Items 1 and 3 of Article 3 of Caishui [2013] No. 103 have been repealed (bridged by Caishui [2018] No. 164); at withdrawal it's taxed at the "wages and salaries" rate; applies only where the employer has established an annuity—individuals can't enroll on their own.

### 10. Products you can hold in a private pension: favor low-fee index
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: When picking products inside the account, prioritize low fees, broad-market, and long-term holding; avoid chasing high-fee active products.
- In plain terms: Don't buy recklessly just because you're in a pension account. The tax break belongs to the account, but product fees you still must pick the low tier yourself—otherwise the tax saving gets eaten by fees.
- Payoff: Within the account you can invest in savings deposits, wealth-management products, commercial pension insurance, publicly offered funds, etc. (Guo Ban Fa [2022] No. 7), but fee differences inside are huge: actively managed equity funds often charge 1.5%+ in annual management plus custody fees, while broad-market index ETFs charge only about 0.2%. At an average account value of 100,000 yuan, held 20 years, with a 1.3% fee gap, fees alone erode about 100,000×[(1+0.013)^20−1] ≈ 29,600 yuan—enough to cancel the tax benefit from the 12,000 yuan annual contribution. Conclusion: prioritize low-fee, broad-market, long-term index or target-date products; don't let "fees" eat "taxes."
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: General Office of the State Council (2022). Opinions on Promoting the Development of Private Pensions (Guo Ban Fa [2022] No. 7, investment-scope clauses). <https://www.ndrc.gov.cn/fggz/jyysr/jysrsbxf/202205/t20220519_1324824.html>; Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (2022). Announcement on Individual Income Tax Policy Concerning Private Pension (Ministry of Finance & STA Announcement 2022 No. 34). <https://szs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202211/t20221104_3849838.htm>
- Note: Product risk is borne by the individual; low fees don't mean low risk—match your risk tolerance and years to retirement; as retirement nears, gradually reduce equity exposure.

### 11. Charitable donations can offset tax—but don't donate just for that
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Donate through a qualified public-welfare social organization or a county-level-or-above government, and obtain and keep the donation receipt for 5 years.
- In plain terms: If you were going to donate anyway, use compliant channels and keep the receipt to offset tax; forcing a donation just to get a deduction doesn't pay. The order is always "decide whether to donate first, then how to deduct."
- Payoff: Individuals who donate through domestic public-welfare organizations or county-level-or-above governments to education, poverty alleviation, hardship relief, and other charitable causes can deduct the portion of donations not exceeding 30% of taxable income; where the State Council prescribes full deduction (e.g., specific public-welfare foundations), follow that (Individual Income Tax Law Article 6; Ministry of Finance & STA Announcement 2019 No. 99). Example: annual comprehensive-income taxable income of 500,000 yuan, donating 150,000 yuan (exactly 30%) is fully deductible; at a 45% marginal rate you save 67,500 yuan, at 20% you save 30,000 yuan. The deduction base is the taxable income "before deducting the donation."
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (2019). Announcement on Individual Income Tax Policy for Public-Welfare and Charitable Donations (Ministry of Finance & STA Announcement 2019 No. 99, Articles 2–3). <https://www.chinatax.gov.cn/chinatax/n810341/n810765/c101653/202001/c5149328/content.html>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). Individual Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China (Article 6). <https://sichuan.chinatax.gov.cn/art/2018/10/30/art_2683_100535.html>
- Note: Donations of equity/real estate are valued at original property value; donations via unqualified platforms (e.g., personal fundraising links) or non-public-welfare earmarked donations aren't deductible; resident individuals may choose the deduction order among comprehensive, classified, and business income.

### 12. Inheritance and gift tax boundaries: no estate tax yet
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=family -->
- Cost: Understand the current rules, don't fall for "estate tax is coming" anxiety marketing, and calculate deed tax and stamp duty clearly before transfer.
- In plain terms: Don't be scared by "transfer now to avoid estate tax"—there's no estate tax in China yet. But property gifts/inheritance still carry transaction taxes and fees; calculate the cost before transfer.
- Payoff: China currently levies no estate tax or gift tax; statutory heirs inheriting real estate or vehicles pay no estate tax (12348 Qinghai Legal Net; Deed Tax Law Article 6 exempts statutory heirs from deed tax on inherited land and housing). But transfers still incur real taxes and fees: non-statutory heirs receiving bequests pay deed tax as a gift (3%–5%, 3% in some cities); inherited/gifted property pays stamp duty on the property-transfer document (0.05%); if inherited property is later resold and doesn't meet "five-years-and-only-home," individual income tax may be 20% on the gain or 1% on the full amount (12366). So what you really must calculate clearly are deed tax, stamp duty, and the future resale's individual income tax cost.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Qinghai Public Legal Services (2025). Inheriting parents' cars and houses (tax FAQ). <https://qh.12348.gov.cn/pub/qhfw/zxfw/dhal/202507/t20250715_126642.html>; State Taxation Administration 12366 (consultation). Taxes involved in property inheritance and gifting. <https://12366.chinatax.gov.cn/nszx/onlinemessage/detail?id=9c81be77b91c494399e7470f4adbe3e4>
- Note: The "five-years-and-only-home" exemption from individual income tax on resale is a key planning point; local deed-tax and stamp-duty details follow the local tax authority, and the online "20% estate tax" claim has no legal basis.

### 13. Overseas financial assets and CRS information exchange
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: When opening an overseas account, truthfully fill in the "tax resident status declaration," and understand your CRS reporting obligations.
- In plain terms: Hiding money in an overseas account to dodge tax is basically no longer realistic. CRS makes tax info flow between countries; compliant reporting is the only right path.
- Payoff: CRS (Common Reporting Standard) was released by the OECD in 2014; China committed to it in 2014, implemented the Measures for the Administration of Due Diligence on Non-Resident Financial Account Tax-Related Information in 2017 (STA Announcement 2017 No. 14), and began exchanging with over 100 jurisdictions in September 2018. Financial institutions annually identify non-resident accounts and report balances, interest, dividends, and financial-asset sale proceeds, automatically exchanging them to the tax-resident country. As of April 2025, 170 jurisdictions worldwide have committed and China has 123 exchange partners (STA English site). This means using overseas accounts to hide assets and dodge tax is essentially no longer feasible; meanwhile "tax residency" is based on residency traces, not merely passport nationality.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Taxation Administration, Ministry of Finance, People's Bank of China, CBRC, CSRC, CIRC (2017). Announcement on Issuing the Measures for the Administration of Due Diligence on Non-Resident Financial Account Tax-Related Information (STA Announcement 2017 No. 14). <https://www.chinatax.gov.cn/chinatax/n810214/c102374/c102377/c102378/c2990907/content.html>; State Taxation Administration (English site). China's Exchange of Information Network / CRS Implementation. <https://www.chinatax.gov.cn/eng/c101270/c101275/c5157937/content.html>
- Note: In China's version of CRS the reporting entity is the financial institution, not the individual; "tax resident" ≠ nationality—the 183-day rule etc. are in the Individual Income Tax Law and tax treaties; deliberately hiding identity or assets may trigger anti-avoidance and legal liability, so always report truthfully in cross-border investing.
