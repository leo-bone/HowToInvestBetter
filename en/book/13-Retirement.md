# XIII. Retirement

Retirement is the investment goal with the longest cycle, the worst certainty, yet the one you least can gamble on. Its core contradiction: after retirement there's no wage cash flow, yet you must cover expenses that may last 30 years, while in that span you simultaneously face four risks—inflation erosion, market volatility, disability care, and "living too long." This chapter sells no product; it only helps you nail down three things: "how much to save, what to save with, and how not to crash midway." At the institutional level, China has built a "three-pillar" system: the first pillar basic pension insurance (a floor, but replacement rate only about 40%), the second pillar enterprise / occupational annuities (narrow coverage), and the third pillar private pension account (piloted in 36 cities in 2022, rolled out nationwide in December 2024, annual contribution cap 12,000 yuan, 3% tax on withdrawal). At the tool level, target-date funds, pension savings, commercial retirement annuities, and long-term care insurance are the four puzzle pieces. Treat social security as a floor not a crutch, use tax-advantaged accounts and equity assets to fill the gap, and use annuities and care insurance to cut off tail risk—that's the steady solution for ordinary people.

Replacement rates, life expectancy, and fund revenue/expenditure come from Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security / National Health Commission / Chinese Academy of Social Sciences public statistics; tax preferences and delayed retirement come from official documents of the State Council, Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration, CSRC, and the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress; the 4% rule comes from Bengen (1994) and the Trinity study; the U.S. comparison uses IRS 2024 limits. ### 1. Open a private pension account, take the tax advantage first
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Contribute at most 12,000 yuan a year (system since 2022, can invest in pension wealth-management / funds / deposits / insurance, four categories); account opening and operation take about half an hour, and funds are generally locked until retirement (withdrawable in very few cases).
- In plain terms: This is a legal tax-advantaged opening the state gives you, annual cap 12,000 yuan, only a 3% skim when you draw. The higher your tax bracket, the more you should use it; even if your bracket is already 3%, don't disdain it—you're picking up tax-free compounding for free. Open the account with your eyes closed, pick a low-fee target-date pension fund or a deposit.
- Payoff: The private pension uses an individual account, full accumulation, closed operation; participants must already be enrolled in urban-worker or urban-rural resident basic pension insurance (State Council Office [2022] No. 7). At the contribution stage, up to 12,000 yuan/year is deducted in full from comprehensive or business income; at the investment stage, in-account gains are temporarily untaxed; at the withdrawal stage, a separate 3% tax applies and is not folded into comprehensive income (Cai Shui [2022] No. 34, extended nationwide by 2024 No. 21, fully implemented from 2024-12-15). For those in the 10%+ marginal individual-income-tax bracket, the contribution deduction alone saves up to 1,200 yuan a year, and more importantly the "tax-free compounding" during the investment period amplifies with the years; the 3% bracket is nearly neutral but still earns long-run tax-free growth. The account's "long-money" attribute also inversely suppresses frequent trading. By June 2024 over 60 million people nationwide had enrolled, the system already the core of the third pillar.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of the State Council (2022), "Opinions on Promoting the Development of Private Pensions" (State Council Office [2022] No. 7, Articles 3, 4, 5: individual account, 12,000 yuan/year cap, can invest in savings / wealth-management / insurance / funds). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/2022-04/21/content_5686402.htm>; Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (2022), "Announcement on Individual Income Tax Policies Concerning Private Pensions" (Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration Announcement 2022 No. 34, Article 1: contribution deduction, investment tax exemption, 3% withdrawal). <https://szs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202211/t20221104_3849838.htm>; Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration (2024), "Announcement on Implementing Private Pension Individual Income Tax Preferences Nationwide" (Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration Announcement 2024 No. 21). <https://shanghai.chinatax.gov.cn/gate/big5/shanghai.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfw/zcfgk/grsds/202412/t474540.html>
- Note: Limitation: the quota is small (12,000 yuan/year) and locked until withdrawal, and the tax-saving power correlates positively with the marginal bracket; the 3% bracket purely earns tax-free compounding but the deduction is barely felt. Cross-reference: Entries 2, 3.

### 2. The "4% rule" helps you estimate how much to save for retirement
<!-- tags: money=0 time=little will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: The premise of understanding it (just read it through once).
- In plain terms: A repeatedly validated empirical anchor: saving "25× your annual spending" is basically enough for retirement (the "25× rule"). It's not iron law—it breaks under early Great Depression or lifespans over 30 years—but as a first approximation of "how much do I actually need to save," it's enough.
- Payoff: In 1994 Bengen backtested U.S. 1926–1976 stock-bond historical data, proposing withdrawing 4% of the portfolio in the first retirement year, then adjusting up by inflation each year; under a 50%–75% stock + rest bonds portfolio, across all 30-year windows in history the money never ran out—he called this highest "safe" withdrawal rate SAFEMAX. That is, "target annual spending ÷ 4% ≈ principal needed," equivalent to saving 25× annual spending. Example: spend 120,000 yuan/year → need about 3 million yuan. The 1998 Trinity study (Cooley et al.) independently verified: 50/50 stock-bond, 4% withdrawal, 30-year success rate 95%, 98% at 75% stocks. Three caveats: first, taxes/fees and advisor fees aren't deducted, so the real safety line is lower (about 3.5%–3.8%); second, it only covers 30 years—if you retire at 60 and expect to live to 95, calculate on 35 years, withdrawal rate should drop to about 3.3%–3.5%; third, it relies on strong U.S. market conditions—Pfau estimates other countries' safety line is only about 2.4%–3%.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Bengen, W. P. (1994), Determining Withdrawal Rates Using Historical Data, Journal of Financial Planning (first-year 4% inflation-adjusted, 30 years not exhausted, 50%–75% stock portfolio). <https://www.web.stanford.edu/~wfsharpe/retecon/4percent.pdf> (Sharpe's review of the paper, with Bengen's own quoted words); Wikipedia (no year), 4% rule (including Trinity Study 1998 success rate). <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4%25_rule>
- Note: Limitation: U.S. market conditions are peculiar, taxes/fees not counted; lifespans over 30 years or a 1968-style high-inflation start break it. Grade A: method verifiable, independently verified multiple times by Trinity et al., but the conclusion is sensitive to country and fees. Cross-reference: Entries 5, 11.

### 3. Don't treat the social-security replacement rate as your crutch
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: The psychological cost of accepting reality.
- In plain terms: Social security is "a floor, not a fortune." To retire without a downgrade, you must fill the gap yourself with private pension, savings, and investing. Don't wait until near retirement to find the hole.
- Payoff: The World Bank's Averting the Old Age Crisis (1994) proposed a retirement-income replacement-rate target of about 70% of pre-retirement wage (net final-year wage caliber); the OECD also uses 70% as the benchmark for maintaining pre-retirement living standards; the ILO's 1952 Social Security (Minimum Standards) Convention (No. 102) sets the public-pension replacement-rate floor at 40%–55%. China's system-design target replacement rate was once 59.2%, but Zheng Bingwen, director of CASS's World Social Security Research Center, pointed out in 2023 that the actual replacement rate of domestic employee pension insurance is about 40%, about 44% five years prior and about 50% fifteen years prior, in continuous decline; in 2023 urban-worker basic pension insurance covered about 142 million retirees with fund expenditure about 6.38 trillion yuan, and under pay-as-you-go the dependency ratio worsens (retirees may exceed 400 million in 2035), further pressing down the replacement rate. Relying only on social security, post-retirement living standards will very likely step down markedly—the gap needs the third pillar to fill.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: World Bank (1994), Averting the Old Age Crisis: Policies to Protect the Old and Promote Growth (three pillars, replacement-rate target). <https://www.ageing.ox.ac.uk/download/312> (review citing the report); International Labour Organization (1952), Social Security (Minimum Standards) Convention (No. 102, pension replacement-rate floor). <https://www.ilo.org/zh-hans/media/261486/download>; Zheng Bingwen (2024-04), "Young People Should Prepare for Retirement Early" (replacement rate about 40%, 44% five years prior, 50% fifteen years prior). <https://static.nfnews.com/content/202404/03/c8751084.html>
- Note: Replacement-rate calibers differ across sources (system-average vs individual replacement rate); CASS data leans toward the "average wage of on-the-job workers" caliber; graded C due to large caliber disputes. Cross-reference: Entries 1, 8.

### 4. Build a "safety cushion" for retirement money before talking growth
<!-- tags: money=0 time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Giving up the chance to chase high returns with retirement principal.
- In plain terms: When young you can stomach volatility; near retirement, still fully invested in stocks—if the opening drops three years straight, principal is permanently dented and there's no income to refill it, the worst case. The closer to retirement, the more you should lock "money you definitely need to spend" into something stable.
- Payoff: In the 5–10 years before retirement you should gradually move "money corresponding to necessary spending" into low-volatility assets like deposits, government bonds, pure bonds, and annuities, reducing "sequence-of-returns risk": at the same average annualized, if the early retirement years drop consecutively, principal is sold at low prices and the later rebound can't make it back, final bankruptcy probability significantly higher than in calm markets. The CSRC's "Guidelines for Pension Target Securities Investment Funds (Trial)" (CSRC Announcement [2018] No. 2), Article 4, explicitly requires target-date funds to "gradually lower the equity allocation ratio and raise the non-equity ratio as the target date approaches," exactly using the system to harden this discipline. In other words, the glide path isn't conservative—it uses time-diversification of "stocks first, bonds later" to dodge the fatal drop at the retirement critical point. On the data, those still fully in stocks at 55, if they hit a 2000- or 2008-style opening, see a markedly higher probability of retiring-at-a-loss.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: China Securities Regulatory Commission (2018), Guidelines for Pension Target Securities Investment Funds (Trial) (CSRC Announcement [2018] No. 2, Article 4: target-date strategy gradually lowers equity ratio as it approaches). <https://big5.www.gov.cn/gate/big5/www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5303451.htm>; General Office of the State Council (2022), "Opinions on Promoting the Development of Private Pensions" (State Council Office [2022] No. 7, third-pillar multi-pillar allocation). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/2022-04/21/content_5686402.htm>
- Note: Limitation: lowering volatility too early sacrifices long-run returns; the break-even point depends on retirement age and risk tolerance. Cross-reference: Entries 2, 11.

### 5. Longevity risk: you're alive, but the money's gone
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: The restraint of reserving a buffer for "living too long."
- In plain terms: People live longer now; retirement money calculated on 20 years won't be enough. Either save more, or buy some annuity that "pays as long as you live" to cut off the tail risk.
- Payoff: The National Health Commission's "2023 Statistical Bulletin on the Development of China's Health Care" shows China's average life expectancy has reached 78.6 years, further rising to 79 in 2024, and women and urban populations live even longer; CASS's Zheng Bingwen points out that in 2035 those aged 60+ will exceed 400 million. If you retire at 60 and live to 90, the retirement period is 30 years—by the "4% rule" you're already near the boundary; living to 95 means dropping the withdrawal rate to about 3.3%. This is "longevity risk": the model assumes 30 years, but you live 35, with no money for the second half. Three hedges: first, save on a 30–35 year basis, not 20 (annual spending ×25 is only the floor); second, keep some social-security and annuity cash flows that "pay as long as you live"; third, use a deferrable annuity to shift tail uncertainty to the insurer.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: National Health Commission (2024), 2023 Statistical Bulletin on the Development of China's Health Care (average life expectancy 78.6 years). <https://big5.www.gov.cn/gate/big5/www.gov.cn/lianbo/bumen/202408/content_6971252.htm>; National Health Commission (2025), Average life expectancy reaches 79 years (2024). <https://cpc.people.com.cn/n1/2025/0313/c64387-40437889.html>; Zheng Bingwen (2024-04), "Young People Should Prepare for Retirement Early" (60+ population exceeds 400 million in 2035). <https://static.nfnews.com/content/202404/03/c8751084.html>
- Note: Limitation: life expectancy is a group average, individual differences are large; graded C because it's a trend inference. Cross-reference: Entries 2, 6.

### 6. Annuities turn "uncertain lifespan" into certain cash flow
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: A premium (or account conversion), with liquidity gone to zero.
- In plain terms: An annuity's essence is you selling the "living too long" risk to an insurer. It's not glamorous, returns unremarkable, but it ensures you won't worry about food at 90. Suitable as the "floor" piece of the retirement portfolio, not all of it.
- Payoff: The essence of commercial retirement annuities / lifetime annuities is "longevity-risk securitization": you pay premiums lump-sum or in installments, the insurer commits to fixed monthly payments per actuarial promise, matching your lifespan, specially treating "fear of living too long." For ordinary people without a stable pension source, it removes the worst tail risk—as long as you're alive, you have money. Under the private-pension framework, commercial pension insurance is explicitly one of the four included product categories (State Council Office [2022] No. 7), and the CSRC's "Pension Target Fund Guidelines" also allows target-risk strategy products as a steady puzzle piece. Note: annuities have poor liquidity, early death may "return principal without returning cost" (unless with death-benefit / guaranteed-payout clauses), and fixed payments don't beat inflation, so it should be only part of the retirement portfolio (e.g., 20%–40%), paired with social security and equity assets, not bet all on it.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: General Office of the State Council (2022), "Opinions on Promoting the Development of Private Pensions" (State Council Office [2022] No. 7, Article 4: can invest in commercial pension insurance). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/2022-04/21/content_5686402.htm>; China Securities Regulatory Commission (2018), Guidelines for Pension Target Securities Investment Funds (Trial) (CSRC Announcement [2018] No. 2, Article 4: target-risk strategy). <https://big5.www.gov.cn/gate/big5/www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5303451.htm>
- Note: Limitation: early-death loss, no inflation hedge, liquidity to zero; graded B because product clauses vary widely. Cross-reference: Entries 5, 7.

### 7. Long-term care is retirement's biggest uncertain bill
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: The awareness to plan ahead.
- In plain terms: The most money-burning part of retirement isn't eating or travel—it's care after losing independence. Social security covers little of this; either buy care insurance or keep a pot of liquid cash always accessible—don't lock it all in real estate.
- Payoff: The seventh census shows China's 60+ exceeds 264 million, with over 40 million disabled / semi-disabled elderly; in 2023 that group was about 45 million. After losing independence, institutional or home care often costs thousands of yuan a month, and social security reimbursement is limited. Since 2016 the state launched long-term care insurance (LTCI, the "sixth insurance") in 15 cities, expanded to 49 cities in 2020, covering over 180 million people, with cumulatively over 2.35 million disabled elderly receiving benefits, and the fund paying about 70% of care costs; People's Daily disclosed the severely disabled's monthly out-of-pocket care dropped from 2,100 yuan to about 600 yuan. But LTCI is still in pilot, with pooling and assessment standards not yet unified nationally, and it only covers "basic" care. For ordinary people: join LTCI, in middle age buy a commercial care / critical-illness policy, and keep a pot of high-liquidity cash—don't lock all retirement money in real estate or long-maturity annuities.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: China Youth Net (2024-10), "Eight Years of LTCI Pilot Show Significant Results" (covers 180 million, 2.35 million receiving benefits, fund pays about 70%). <https://news.youth.cn/hotnews_41880/202410/t20241028_15609485.htm>; People's Daily Online (2016-11), "Establishing Long-Term Care Insurance Is Urgent" (overall fund payment level about 70%, monthly out-of-pocket from 2,100 to 600). <https://health.people.com.cn/n1/2016/1127/c408579-28899778.html>; China Daily Net (2021-11), "What Is the 'Sixth Insurance'" (49-city pilot, over 40 million disabled / semi-disabled). <https://qiye.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202111/02/WS61809cbaa3107be4979f6057.html>
- Note: Limitation: LTCI is not yet nationally finalized, benefits and assessment vary by region; graded C because data are mostly pilot statistics. Cross-reference: Entries 6, 12.

### 8. Retirement age will only keep getting later
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Accepting the trend of delayed retirement.
- In plain terms: Don't plan on "retiring exactly at 60." The policy direction is to push it back; your reserve plan must be squeezed from both sides—retire later and retire longer.
- Payoff: On September 13, 2024 the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress passed the "Decision on Implementing the Progressive Delay of the Statutory Retirement Age," and approved the "Measures of the State Council on the Progressive Delay of the Statutory Retirement Age," effective 2025-01-01: over 15 years, raise male employees from 60 to 63, original 55-year-old female employees to 58, original 50-year-old female employees to 55 (male employees and 55-year females by 1 month every 4 months, 50-year females by 1 month every 2 months); simultaneously from 2030-01-01 over 10 years, raise the minimum contribution years for monthly pension receipt from 15 to 20 years (add 6 months a year; those retiring 2025–2029 still follow 15 years). Supporting flexible retirement: having met minimum contribution years you may voluntarily retire up to 3 years early (not below the original statutory age), or by agreement delay up to 3 years. Meaning for individuals: the receipt year is pushed back, the contribution years lengthened, and the retirement-reserve window is compressed—planning on "retire at 60 + 30-year retirement" overestimates the gap, while planning on later retirement requires more accumulation during working years.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2024), "Decision on Implementing the Progressive Delay of the Statutory Retirement Age" (passed 2024-09-13, with State Council measures, effective 2025-01-01). <https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202409/content_6974294.htm>; NPC Website (2024-09), "Steadily and Orderly Implement the Progressive Delay of the Statutory Retirement Age" (MHRSS interpretation: male 60→63, female 55→58 / 50→55, minimum contribution years 15→20). <http://www.npc.gov.cn/c2/c30834/202409/t20240914_439685.html>
- Note: Limitation: flexible retirement needs employer agreement, special occupations have separate policies; graded A as a legally effective document. Cross-reference: Entries 3, 5.

### 9. Don't bet all retirement on a house
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: The awareness to diversify allocation.
- In plain terms: One house can't serve as pension—you can't "eat the house" month by month. When you really need money, selling is slow and may hit a low. Retirement money needs a part placed where it's always accessible.
- Payoff: Real estate is "paper wealth," not cash flow: it's hard to liquidate (transaction cycles measured in months, with intermediary fee/tax friction), hard to split (can't take 1/12 of a unit a month), and prices are cyclical—when you urgently need cash you may be forced to sell cheap. The World Bank's Averting the Old Age Crisis (1994) stresses retirement relies on "multiple pillars"—public pension, mandatory savings, voluntary savings / commercial insurance layered—exactly opposing betting safety on a single asset. For individuals, retirement cash flow should have at least part in financial assets you can draw monthly (deposits, bonds, target-date pension funds, annuities); real estate can be a backstop net asset, but shouldn't be the only source. Especially in retirement, if you hit large long-term-care or medical expenses, real estate's liquidity shortfall amplifies financial risk. The sensible approach is a "real estate + financial assets + insurance" triad, ensuring sustainable monthly cash inflow.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: World Bank (1994), Averting the Old Age Crisis (multiple pillars, opposing single-asset dependence). <https://www.ageing.ox.ac.uk/download/312>; General Office of the State Council (2022), "Opinions on Promoting the Development of Private Pensions" (State Council Office [2022] No. 7, third-pillar multi-pillar allocation). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/2022-04/21/content_5686402.htm>
- Note: Limitation: core-location prime real estate has better liquidity; the conclusion is a general rule; graded C as an allocation principle. Cross-reference: Entries 7, 10.

### 10. Inflation is retirement's invisible killer—look at real returns
<!-- tags: money=0 time=little will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: One extra step when accounting: "subtract inflation."
- In plain terms: You think parking money in the bank is safe, but 30 years later that money buys less of the same things. Retirement money can't all sit in deposits—keep a part that can keep up with inflation.
- Payoff: Long-run 2%–3% mild inflation halves purchasing power in 30 years: 1 yuan at 2.5% inflation is only worth about 0.47 yuan after 30 years (1÷1.025³⁰≈0.47). Bengen's 4% rule is an "real (inflation-adjusted)" withdrawal, itself acknowledging inflation keeps eating principal; if the portfolio is all deposits, nominal value is preserved but real purchasing power shrinks yearly. Historical data shows stocks / broad-market indices have a long-run real annualized about 6%–7%, clearly beating deposits and inflation; government bonds and TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities) can partly hedge. China's CPI in 1990–2023 was mostly in the 2%–3% range; the retirement portfolio must contain assets "that keep up with inflation," otherwise over a 30-year retirement you're loose early and tight late, forced to tighten in old age. One line: look at real returns, not just the book.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Bengen, W. P. (1994), Determining Withdrawal Rates Using Historical Data (4% is an inflation-adjusted real withdrawal rate). <https://www.web.stanford.edu/~wfsharpe/retecon/4percent.pdf>; World Bank (1994), Averting the Old Age Crisis (inflation's erosion of old-age security). <https://www.ageing.ox.ac.uk/download/312>
- Note: Limitation: equity assets hedge inflation but swing short-term, need to match age (see Entry 11); graded B because long-run patterns are clear but short-term disturbed. Cross-reference: Entries 4, 11.

### 11. The older you get, the more you cut volatility (glide path)
<!-- tags: money=0 time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Part of the potential high returns when young.
- In plain terms: Fully invested in stocks at 25 is fine; still fully invested at 55 is gambling with your life. The older you get, the more you move "money you definitely need to spend" into something stable—it's buying sleep with discipline.
- Payoff: Automatically lowering the stock ratio with age (target-date / glide path) is a discipline hardened by regulation and verified by academia. The CSRC's "Guidelines for Pension Target Securities Investment Funds (Trial)" (No. [2018] 2), Article 4, forcibly requires target-date funds to "gradually lower the equity allocation ratio and raise the non-equity ratio as the set target date approaches." The principle is "sequence-of-returns risk": if the 3–5 years before retirement hit a sharp drop, principal is sold at low prices and the later rebound can barely recover it, bankruptcy probability spikes; while more stocks when young and more bonds near retirement moves the most fatal volatility to the early years with enough time to recover. Backtests show lowering volatility near retirement significantly reduces the "retire-at-a-loss" probability. For individuals not using funds, you can directly use the "age rule": equity ratio ≈ 100 (or 110) minus age—about 45%–55% equity at 55, dropping to 35%–45% at 65.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: China Securities Regulatory Commission (2018), Guidelines for Pension Target Securities Investment Funds (Trial) (CSRC Announcement [2018] No. 2, Article 4: target-date glide path forcibly lowers equity). <https://big5.www.gov.cn/gate/big5/www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5303451.htm>; Bengen, W. P. (1994), Determining Withdrawal Rates Using Historical Data (portfolio adjusts stock-bond with age, lowers risk). <https://www.web.stanford.edu/~wfsharpe/retecon/4percent.pdf>
- Note: Limitation: raising the bond ratio too early sacrifices compounding; "100 − age" is only a rough starting point. Cross-reference: Entries 4, 10.

### 12. Insure your parents and yourself with medical insurance first, before investing
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=family -->
- Cost: A few thousand to over ten thousand yuan a year in premiums.
- In plain terms: One illness for your parents can empty your retirement principal. Put up the medical-insurance firewall first; talk about growth after. Get the order wrong and all the growth is paper riches.
- Payoff: A serious illness's out-of-pocket part can instantly swallow years of savings: basic medical insurance has a deductible, a cap, and a co-pay ratio, with targeted drugs, proton/heavy-ion therapy, ICU, and much more outside the formulary. Long-term care is retirement's biggest uncertain bill—China's disabled / semi-disabled elderly already about 45 million, institutional / home care thousands of yuan a month, while social security coverage is limited. The national long-term care insurance (the "sixth insurance") pilot covers 180 million people, cumulatively 2.35 million disabled elderly receiving benefits, the fund paying about 70% of care costs, but it's still not nationally finalized. For the family, first use million-medical / Huiminbao / cancer insurance to block the "poverty-from-illness" tail risk, then consider retirement growth—the order must never reverse: before protection is adequate, any "compounding growth" can go to zero before one hospitalization bill.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: China Youth Net (2024-10), "Eight Years of LTCI Pilot Show Significant Results" (covers 180 million, 2.35 million receiving benefits, pays about 70%). <https://news.youth.cn/hotnews_41880/202410/t20241028_15609485.htm>; People's Daily Online (2016-11), "Establishing Long-Term Care Insurance Is Urgent" (overall fund payment level about 70%, monthly out-of-pocket from 2,100 to 600). <https://health.people.com.cn/n1/2016/1127/c408579-28899778.html>
- Note: Limitation: health insurance has health declarations and waiting periods, hard to buy at older ages; graded C as a risk principle, not statistics. Cross-reference: Entry 7.
