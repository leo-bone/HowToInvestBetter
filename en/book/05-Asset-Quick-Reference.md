# V. Asset Quick Reference

This chapter is a **pure fact sheet**. It only includes historically verifiable data and conclusions already published by authoritative institutions (long-run returns, volatility, rules, risk disclosures), and represents no buy/sell advice. Each entry is broken down by "what it costs you / what it returns / how hard the evidence is," with one goal: build a sense of the *order of magnitude* of each asset's return and risk before you decide whether and how much to allocate. Market returns shift with interest rates, valuations, and inflation—history is not a promise for the future; every figure is tagged with its source year and scope so you can check it yourself.

We deliberately avoid any "what to buy now" timing call here. Lesson one of asset allocation is to load the long-run nature of assets into your head (equities ≈ high growth, high volatility; bonds ≈ low growth, low volatility; gold ≈ store-of-value hedge; cash ≈ liquidity), then assemble a portfolio from your own risk tolerance and horizon—not chase whatever won the past year.

### 1. Broad-market index ETF (CSI 300 / CSI 500 / S&P 500, etc.)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Expense ratio 0.03%–0.5%/year + a one-time account opening; ETF trading commission about 0.005%–0.03% (per 10,000), stamp duty exempt, almost no other friction.
- In plain terms: The flagship asset. Don't pick stocks, don't time the market—buy a low-cost index covering the whole market and invest monthly. It won't make you rich overnight, but it parks you firmly on the "long-term winner" side.
- Payoff: Over the long run, a low-cost broad-market index covering the whole market is the most worry-free core holding. The S&P 500 with dividends has returned about 10.0% annualized since 1928 (geometric mean; about 6.9% real after inflation), and across nearly 97 years not a single 20-year rolling window was negative (NYU Stern / Damodaran, 1928–2025). The CSI 300 total-return index returned about 9.9% annualized in 2005–2025, tracking its ROE center of ~12.6%; its ten-year annualized return was 8.78% from Aug 2013 to Jul 2023 (China Securities Index). The key isn't the gain—it's "beating most active products for sure": S&P Dow Jones's SPIVA shows about 92% of U.S. large-cap active funds underperformed the benchmark over 20 years, about 94% of all active equity funds, and about 65% even within 3 years; in Buffett's ten-year bet (2008–2017), the low-cost S&P 500 index fund returned 125.8% cumulative (8.5% annualized), beating the five fund-of-funds average of 36.3% (about 3.1% annualized).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: S&P Dow Jones Indices (2024). SPIVA U.S. Scorecard (about 92% of large-cap active funds underperformed over 20 years). <https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-australia/>; China Securities Index Co., Ltd. (2023). "Indexing Investment New Observations | Breaking 300 Billion: Investors Turn to China's Flagship Equity Index" (CSI 300 total return Aug 2013–Jul 2023 annualized 8.78%). <https://www.csindex.com.cn/#/about/newsDetail?id=14867>; Vanguard (2026). How index funds can help Americans build wealth (index fee 0.09% vs active 0.56%; 10,000 USD invested in 1976 worth about 2 million USD by 2026). <https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/indexing_benefits.pdf>
- Note: The long-run win rate comes from three factors—zero-sum game + fee drag + scarcity of persistence (Vanguard, "The case for low-cost index-fund investing"); but a broad-market index still carries the whole market's systematic volatility—the S&P 500 fell 37% in 2008 and A-shares swing even harder, so smooth it with dollar-cost averaging and long holding. It's a core holding, not "risk-free."

### 2. Government bonds / interest-rate bonds (the risk-free benchmark)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Opportunity cost (giving up higher returns); the 10-year treasury yield has been about 1.5%–2.5% in recent years (2024–2026), and holding to maturity carries almost no credit risk.
- In plain terms: The treasury yield is the yardstick for "risk-free." Any product that dares to out-yield it by a wide margin while claiming no risk is simply hiding that extra spread as risk premium.
- Payoff: Treasuries and interest-rate bonds are the ballast of a portfolio—principal-safe, used for emergency funds and volatility reduction. The U.S. 10-year treasury returned about 4.79% nominal annualized over 1928–2024, but only about 1.8% real (inflation-adjusted), and fell as much as 17.8% in a single year in 2022 (SmartAsset based on NYU Stern). In China, the ChinaBond Treasury Total Index returned about 4.3% annualized over 2005–2025, versus the Wind All-A at about 10.6%, real estate at about 8.3%, and CSI 300 at about 9.9% (Guosheng Securities, Xun Yugen). It won't make you rich, but it delivers three things: a certain coupon, low/negative correlation with stocks in most periods (often a hedge in crises), and "ammunition" for stock-bond rebalancing. When rates rise, bond prices fall; the longer the duration, the bigger the swing—use short duration or hold-to-maturity to dodge it.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Guosheng Securities, Xun Yugen (2026). "What Is a Reasonable Return on Stocks?" (ChinaBond Treasury Total Index annualized 4.3% over 2005–2025). <https://finance.sina.cn/2026-06-29/detail-iniezsvf0351433.d.html>; SmartAsset (2025). Stock Index vs Bond Yields Year by Year (U.S. 10-year treasury nominal annualized 4.79% over 1928–2024; -17.8% in 2022). <https://www.smartasset.com/investing/stock-index-vs-bond-yields-year-by-year>; InvestEdge360 (2026). US 10-Year Treasury Yield: 50-Year History Chart (4.21% in 2024, 4.26% in 2025). <https://investedge360.com/blog/us-10-year-treasury-yield-50-year-history-chart>
- Note: A-share treasuries' duration risk is masked by the low-rate environment; the 2022 global bond "rare synchronized sell-off" warns that stock-bond negative correlation isn't forever. It's better used as a "risk-free benchmark" to measure other assets' risk premium, not as the main return engine.

### 3. Money market fund (emergency-fund container)
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Almost zero (T+0 flexible, no subscription/redemption fee); only an opportunity cost—cash parked here isn't invested in equity/debt assets.
- In plain terms: Emergency money isn't for earning—it's for being "available anytime." The money market fund is its right container; don't trade it for stocks, and don't confuse it with a deposit.
- Payoff: About 1.5%–2.5% annualized and withdrawable anytime, to cover 3–6 months of living expenses. Per the *Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Money Market Funds* (CSRC Order No. 120, 2015), money market funds invest only in money-market instruments, with an average remaining maturity ≤ 120 days and a floor on high-liquidity assets—far safer than bank wealth products; but Article 18 explicitly states "buying a money market fund is not a deposit," and the manager guarantees neither profit nor a minimum return. In a rate-cut cycle the 7-day annualized often lands at 1.2%–2.0% (2024–2026), falling as market rates drop—it's essentially a "liquidity premium," not an "investment return."
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: China Securities Regulatory Commission and the People's Bank of China (2015). *Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Money Market Funds* (CSRC Order No. 120; Article 9 average remaining maturity ≤ 120 days; Article 18 no guaranteed principal or return). <https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c106256/c1654015/1654015/files/52c60ebbeb5642e08f9f629bb8ed190e.pdf>; China Securities Journal / China Securities Network (2015). Interpretation of the *Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Money Market Funds* (liquidity and duration rules). <https://www.cs.com.cn/tzjj/jjks/201512/t20151229_4873464.html>
- Note: The 2022 *New Rules for Cash-Management Wealth Products* brought bank cash-management products fully in line with money market funds, and their returns converged; money market funds suit "money you need anytime," solve no long-term growth, and may lag inflation if held long.

### 4. Bond funds / "fixed-income-plus" (lower volatility than stocks)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Expense ratio 0.3%–0.8%/year + credit/rate volatility; the equity portion of the "+" also brings equity drawdowns.
- In plain terms: "Fixed-income-plus" isn't "steady profit." Bond funds can fall too (rates up, credit default), but long-run volatility is far below stocks—the part of the portfolio you can sleep on.
- Payoff: Medium-to-long-run annualized about 3%–6%, with far smaller drawdowns than stocks, used to cushion the portfolio. Over a century-scale window, the U.S. 10-year treasury returned about 4.79% nominal and about 1.8% real (SmartAsset / NYU Stern), while "fixed-income-plus" with equity uses stock-bond negative correlation to suppress volatility and lift the Sharpe ratio. China's ChinaBond Treasury Total Index returned about 4.3% annualized over 2005–2025; bond products add credit and equity on top. Vanguard's research conclusion: stock-bond allocation (e.g., 60/40) significantly cuts a single asset's max drawdown, and rebalancing itself is a discipline of "buy low, sell high." But the "+" part, if it holds stocks/convertibles, has its volatility underestimated.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Guosheng Securities, Xun Yugen (2026). "What Is a Reasonable Return on Stocks?" (ChinaBond Treasury Total Index annualized 4.3% over 2005–2025). <https://finance.sina.cn/2026-06-29/detail-iniezsvf0351433.d.html>; Vanguard (2023). The case for low-cost index-fund investing (stock-bond allocation, diversification lowers volatility). <https://www.vanguard.co.uk/professional/vanguard-365/investment-knowledge/portfolio-construction/the-case-for-indexing>; SmartAsset (2025). Stock Index vs Bond Yields Year by Year (long-run U.S. treasury return and volatility). <https://www.smartasset.com/investing/stock-index-vs-bond-yields-year-by-year>
- Note: [Contested] Some "fixed-income-plus" holds equity, with volatility and drawdown underestimated—read the underlying contract; buying it as a deposit is a common mistake. In the 2022 bond bear market many "fixed-income-plus" products drew down over 5%; check the equity position cap before buying.

### 5. REITs (real estate trusts)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Expense ratio (management + custody about 0.5%–1%) + high volatility (strongly tied to property cycles and rates) + premium/discount and liquidity.
- In plain terms: Want real estate exposure without buying a whole building? REITs are the ticket. But they aren't bonds, and they can fall hard—don't treat them as "high-yield deposits."
- Payoff: The FTSE Nareit All Equity REITs index returned about 10.94% annualized total return over 1972–2025, about 9.43% over 40 years, long-run beating the S&P 500 (about 11.15% over the same period); the current dividend yield is about 4.07%, far above the S&P 500's 1.10% (Nareit 2025 Fact Sheet). It gives real-asset exposure beyond stocks, with low correlation to stocks and bonds and inflation-hedge properties. But volatility isn't low: it drew down clearly with rising rates in 2022. China's public infrastructure REITs launched their first 9 products on June 21, 2021, raising about 31.4 billion yuan, backed by toll roads, industrial parks, warehousing, and sewage treatment; first-week average gain was 2.15%, capitalization rates mostly above 4.5% (China Securities Network), but the sample is only a few years and hasn't fully lived through a cycle.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Nareit (2025). REIT Industry Fact Sheet (FTSE Nareit All Equity REITs 1972–2025 annualized 10.94%, 40-year annualized 9.43%, dividend yield 4.07%). <https://www.reit.com/sites/default/files/2026-01/MediaFactSheet_Dec-2025.pdf>; Xinhua (2021). "First Batch of Public REITs Formally Approved" (CSRC and NDRC launched the pilot on Apr 30, 2020). <https://app.xinhuanet.com/news/article.html?articleId=63f54a73-7d0c-4dc4-bec6-8b633e769b86>; China Securities Network (2021). "Public REITs Expansion Imminent" (first 9 raised 31.4 billion yuan, capitalization rate above 4.5%). <https://cs.com.cn/tzjj/jjdt/202107/t20210730_6189395.html>
- Note: [Contested] Domestic REITs are new, short-sampled, and homogeneous (mostly infrastructure, no residences); the logic isn't fully validated. U.S. REITs are long-run strong but swing hard in short cycles—don't make them a core holding; use a small satellite allocation.

### 6. Gold (a hedge, but it earns no interest)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Storage / ETF expense ratio (about 0.2%–0.4%/year); no cash flow (no interest, no dividend)—holding only yields capital gains.
- In plain terms: Gold's "real return" over centuries is unimpressive; its true value is "it rises when others panic." A 5%–10% allocation as insurance is fine; loading up as an investment is betting on depreciation.
- Payoff: The World Gold Council estimates gold's annualized price gain was about 8% from 1971 (collapse of Bretton Woods) to 2023, with a long-run real return above zero but far below stocks; over the past 20 years RMB-denominated gold returned about 10% annualized, and about 13.6% over the past 10 years (Securities Times citing WGC). Its role is "hedge," not "growth": low correlation with stocks and bonds, often strong in high inflation, geopolitical conflict, or dollar-credit weakness. But it earns no interest, is pressured when real rates rise, and is volatile (it hit 850 USD/oz in 1980 then traded sideways for years). As portfolio insurance, a 5%–10% weight suffices; loading up drags long-run compound return.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: World Gold Council (2024). Gold's long-term expected returns: A building block approach (1971–2023 annualized about 8%, model 8.6%). <https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/golds-long-term-expected-returns/building-block-approach>; Securities Times (2025). "Order Restructuring Underway: Can 'Gold+' Soar?" (1971–2023 annualized about 8%, past 20 years 10.2%, past 10 years 13.6%). <https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/2997848.html>; Nareit / Perth Mint (2025). Gold's role in a modern portfolio (long-run about 8%–9%, allocate 5%–10%). <https://www.perthmint.com/globalassets/assets/documents/superannuation/gold-report-2025.pdf>
- Note: Gold "preserving value" means purchasing power roughly flat, not steady growth; Dalio suggests 10%–15% gold in a portfolio as a hedge, but ordinary people should use a gold ETF to avoid physical storage and premiums.

### 7. Convertible bonds (a bond floor below, equity upside above)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: The effort to study the terms + single-bond credit risk; subscription needs a securities account and market-value/capital tie-up; trading has commissions and stamp duty.
- In plain terms: The "bond floor" isn't an iron floor (it can still break par when the underlying stock plunges). Subscribing to new convertible bonds is OK for beginners; speculating in high-priced convertibles is a different kind of bet.
- Payoff: Convertibles "attack when rising, defend when falling," but after the 2019–2022 speculation their valuations diverged wildly. The SSE's *Implementation Rules for the Issuance of Convertible Corporate Bonds* (SSE Release [2018] No. 115) sets the minimum subscription unit at 1,000 yuan and T-day subscription as non-cancelable; typical clauses include "conditional redemption" (the underlying stock at ≥130% of the conversion price for at least 20 of 30 consecutive days triggers redemption) and "conditional put" (in the last two years, the underlying stock below 70% of the conversion price for 30 consecutive days triggers a put), forming a two-way bond-floor/equity constraint. High-priced convertibles (price >130 yuan) lose their floor protection; after the 2021–2022 speculation drawdowns reached 30%+, with both break-par and forced-redemption coexisting; only low-priced, low-premium convertibles show the "floor below."
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: Shanghai Stock Exchange (2018). *Implementation Rules for the Issuance of Convertible Corporate Bonds by Listed Companies on the Shanghai Stock Exchange* (SSE Release [2018] No. 115, Articles 5/9 subscription rules). <https://big5.sse.com.cn/lawandrules/sselawsrules2025/bond/convertible/issue/c/c_20250609_10781288.shtml>; Shanghai Stock Exchange (2025). Compilation of Convertible Corporate Bond Rules (redemption/put clause templates). <https://big5.sse.com.cn/site/cht/www.sse.com.cn/lawandrules/assemble/c/10116497/files/77fbf7bc013141ff843668080951f781.pdf>
- Note: [Contested] The "bond floor" only holds near par; high-premium convertibles carry risk close to the underlying stock. Subscription gains have narrowed with issuance pace and market conditions, and speculating in high-priced convertibles is essentially a leveraged equity bet—ordinary people should only subscribe to new issues and hold low-priced ones.

### 8. Dividend-low-volatility strategy
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Expense ratio (ETF about 0.2%–0.5%) + possibly lagging high-growth stocks in some periods (e.g., U.S./AI rallies, A-share thematic years).
- In plain terms: Pick a basket of "steady dividend payers, low volatility," and the long-run experience beats chasing hotspots. It's essentially packing the two proven factors "value + low volatility."
- Payoff: Dividend yield about 3%–5% + low volatility, with smaller long-run drawdowns than a broad index—good for the steady hand. The Fama-French three-factor model (1993) shows high book-to-market (value) stocks earn about a 4%/year premium over growth long-run, small caps about a 3%/year premium over large, and low volatility is a separate risk dimension independent of size/value. CSI data shows the CSI 300's average dividend yield was about 2.11% over 2005–2022; dividend-low-volatility indices add low-volatility screening on top, with a better long-run Sharpe. But it's not "no drawdown"—the value factor can lag for years (e.g., growth led 2017–2020); you must stomach style rotation.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Fama, E. & French, K. (1993). Common Risk Factors in the Returns on Stocks and Bonds (value premium about 4%, size premium about 3%). <https://wiki.mbalib.com/zh-tw/Fama-French%E4%B8%89%E5%9B%A0%E5%AD%90%E6%A8%A1%E5%9E%8B>; China Securities Index Co., Ltd. (2023). "Indexing Investment New Observations" (CSI 300 average dividend yield 2.11% over 2005–2022). <https://www.csindex.com.cn/#/about/newsDetail?id=14867>; Vanguard (2023). The case for low-cost index-fund investing (factor premia and low cost). <https://www.vanguard.co.uk/professional/vanguard-365/investment-knowledge/portfolio-construction/the-case-for-indexing>
- Note: Dividend-low-volatility is "experience optimization," not "return domination"; it does well when rates fall and the economy is weak, and lags when inflation rises and growth explodes. Treat it as part of a steady core, not the whole.

### 9. QDII / overseas indices
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Expense ratio (1.0%–1.5%/year, higher than onshore broad-market) + exchange-rate volatility + subscription/redemption lag (about T+7) + quota-premium risk.
- In plain terms: Buying U.S./global indices isn't just about earning overseas—it's about not putting all eggs in one economy. But the exchange rate is an invisible hand; don't treat it as a pure enhancer.
- Payoff: Diversify a single market and share global growth. The S&P 500 total return averaged about 10.7% annualized over 1960–2025 (Guosheng Securities, Xun Yugen), tracking the U.S. market's long-run ROE; MSCI All-Country and other broad indices give cross-country diversification. But QDII has a two-layer discount: first, fees well above onshore ETFs; second, RMB-denominated return = the asset's local-currency return ± exchange-rate move, and two-way rate swings can eat several percentage points, and when quotas are tight the secondary-market price can carry a premium (e.g., U.S.-stock QDIIs premiums exceeded 5% in 2020–2021). It solves "geographic concentration risk," not "return enhancement."
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Guosheng Securities, Xun Yugen (2026). "What Is a Reasonable Return on Stocks?" (S&P 500 total return 1960–2025 annualized 10.7%). <https://finance.sina.cn/2026-06-29/detail-iniezsvf0351433.d.html>; Vanguard (2026). How index funds can help Americans build wealth (global diversification, low-cost indices). <https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/indexing_benefits.pdf>; SlickCharts (2026). S&P 500 Total Returns (annual returns with dividends, 1926–2025). <https://www.slickcharts.com/sp500/returns>
- Note: QDII's value is "not betting everything on A-shares," not higher returns; you bear the exchange-rate risk yourself—long-run USD/RMB swings both ways, so don't treat the rate as a free enhancer. Prefer large, low-tracking-error, quota-ample broad-market QDIIs.

### 10. Reverse repo (pick up pennies at month/quarter end)
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Zero (built into your stock account), with extremely low fees (1-day about 0.001% of turnover, about 1 yuan per 100,000 yuan).
- In plain terms: Cash lying in your stock account—throw a 1-day reverse repo before close and earn a night's interest for free. A zero-cost move; do it on the way by.
- Payoff: Idle cash's short-run annualized偶尔 spikes to 3%–5% (month/quarter/holiday eve), with principal settled by ChinaClear as central counterparty—very low risk. The SSE's *Guidelines No. 2 for General Pledge-Style Repo Trading* sets terms of 1/2/3/4/7/14/28/91/182 days, codes "204***" (GC-prefixed), with the repo party pledging treasuries and multilateral net settlement by the central counterparty. Interest accrues by actual calendar days occupied (smoother after the May 2017 rule change); GC001 is usually 1.5%–2.5% daily, but before the 2024 New Year several terms exceeded 5%, and before the 2025 New Year exceeded 2.5% (Securities Times). The rate locks at trade, and funds return automatically at maturity—a standard "overnight idle cash" move.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: Shanghai Stock Exchange (2022). *Shanghai Stock Exchange Bond Trading Rules Applicable Guidelines No. 2—General Pledge-Style Repo Trading* (Article 13 terms, Article 14 GC codes, Article 25 repurchase-price formula). <https://www.sse.com.cn/lawandrules/sselawsrules2025/bond/trading/currency/c/10781048/files/9c80d6d104164618b79f679aff8de1da.docx>; Securities Times (2025). "New Year Holiday 'Free Money,' Best Window Opens" (above 5% before 2024 New Year, above 2.5% before 2025 New Year). <https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/3562121.html>
- Note: Reverse repo is "picking pennies," not "making a fortune"—rates are only slightly above money market funds normally; only worth a dedicated move when rates jump at month/quarter/holiday eve; otherwise just keep money market / interbank CD funds.

### 11. IPO / new-bond subscription (A-shares)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Market-value threshold (SSE/SZSE holdings) + capital tie-up on winning + break-par loss risk.
- In plain terms: The old "subscription always wins" dividend is narrowing; now pick by fundamentals. Treating it as a free lottery is fine; treating it as steady income no longer holds.
- Payoff: The break-par rate fell from a 2022 peak of about 28% to about 3% in 2024 and near zero in 2025; but "blind subscription" has returned to "pick-and-choose." Securities Times statistics: 2020 break-par rate 5.03%, 2021 4.2%, 2022 28.27%, 2023 16.61%, 2024 about 3.23% (only 1 broke on day one); in the Dragon Year (from Feb 2024) all 22 had no break-par, average gain near 200%; Baidu Baike also lists 2023 first-day break-par 21.41%, 2024 2%, 2025 zero. CITIC Securities' post-"new inquiry rules" registration-based figures: 2021 22.2%, 2022 31.4%, 2023 16.7%, 2024 1.5%, 2025 YTD 0%. Subscription gains now depend heavily on the stock's quality and issue pricing.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: Securities Times (2024). "Upheaval! New-Stock Gains Double, Is Blind Subscription Back?" (2020–2024 break-par rate and first-day gain stats). <https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/1167700.html>; Baidu Baike (2026). Break-par rate (2023 21.41%, 2024 2%, 2025 zero). <https://baike.baidu.com/item/%E7%A0%B4%E5%8F%91%E7%8E%87/69139871>; CITIC Securities (2025). Offline subscription statistics (post-"new inquiry rules" registration-based break-par rates by year). <https://ima.qq.com/wiki/?shareId=39379873440bfb2eb7f27df83bcf73d8223d9cccd3022c1c44fcec6b4b94cf47>
- Note: [Contested] The break-par rate fell as regulators tightened pricing, but "zero break-par" can't be extrapolated as the norm; high-priced new stocks can still lose ten-thousand-plus yuan per lot. Move from "blind subscription" to "check the issue P/E and fundamentals," and bear the market-value base's volatility.

### 12. Options (buyers are long-run negative-sum)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Time value decaying continuously (theta) + zeroing risk + premium.
- In plain terms: Option time value leaks every day; most buyers expire worthless. It's an insurance/hedge tool, not a retail ATM.
- Payoff: Suitable only for professionals hedging/covered calls; mostly a net drain for retail. The option buyer pays a premium for a right, but time value bleeds linearly (accelerating) toward expiry—you lose even if the underlying doesn't move; statistically most expire worthless, a "negative-sum" game (exchange fees + market-maker spreads keep draining capital). The academic and industry consensus: retail buying one-sided options has a negative long-run expectation; only "covered calls" (hold stock, sell calls) or "protective puts" (hold stock, buy insurance) have positive functions. Leverage amplifies gains and losses, but the loss cap is the premium for buyers and can far exceed principal for sellers.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Hull, J. (2017). Options, Futures and Other Derivatives (option pricing and time-value decay). <https://www.ivey.uwo.ca/faculty/JohnHull/>; China Securities Regulatory Commission (2017). *Measures for the Administration of Investor Suitability for Securities and Futures* (CSRC Order No. 130; Article 17 lists high-leverage/principal-erosion products as high-risk). <https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100210/c1498841/1498841/files/1636094382980_55189.pdf>
- Note: [Contested] Covered calls are a usable "enhancement + insurance" for stockholders, but need skill to spot underlying risk; ordinary people betting direction as buyers almost certainly lose long-run—treat it as insurance cost, not investment.

### 13. Structured deposits / bank wealth products (after net-value reform)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Liquidity (opportunity cost of multi-term locks) + hidden fees + possible book-value loss from net-value swings.
- In plain terms: Stop treating it as "high-yield deposit." It no longer guarantees principal—read what's underneath before buying. The bit of extra yield over deposits corresponds to a bit of extra risk.
- Payoff: A bit higher than deposits, but after the Asset Management Rules it no longer guarantees principal and net value moves. In April 2018 the PBOC and three other ministries issued the *Guiding Opinions on Regulating Asset Management Business of Financial Institutions* ("Asset Management Rules"), explicitly "no promise of guaranteed principal and returns, break rigid redemption"; the transition ended Jan 1, 2022, expected-return products exited, and bank wealth products went fully net-valued (People's Daily Online). This means wealth-product returns are no longer fixed; when the underlying holds bonds/non-standard/equity, net value moves with the market, and in extremes can break par. It fits as a cash/short-bond substitute "slightly above deposits, tolerating small swings," but is never a deposit.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: People's Bank of China and four other ministries (2018). *Guiding Opinions on Regulating Asset Management Business of Financial Institutions* (Asset Management Rules, rigid-redemption-breaking clauses). <https://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2022/0101/c1004-32322377.html>; People's Daily / People's Daily Online (2022). "Asset Management Rules Transition Ends, Wealth Products to Break Rigid Redemption." <https://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2022/0101/c1004-32322377.html>
- Note: [Contested] The "performance comparison benchmark" is not an expected return; breaking par is normal after net-valuing. Before buying, read the prospectus's investment scope and risk grade (R2/R3), and ditch the "deposit-like" expectation entirely.

### 14. Insurance wealth / annuity (lock in long-term cash flow)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Liquidity (early surrender loses big, cash value is very low in the first years) + fees (initial, management) + long lock-up.
- In plain terms: An annuity's value is "forced saving + lifetime cash flow," not high return. Calculate the IRR before buying; don't let the "illustrated benefits" dazzle you.
- Payoff: Certain cash flow (retirement/education), but the real internal rate of return (IRR) is often dressed up by sales talk—the industry's actual is about 2%–3.5%. Annuities/pension insurance use "illustration rates" to create a high-return illusion, but illustrations come in low/mid/high tiers and aren't guaranteed; regulators have required more transparent benefit illustrations for participating, universal, and unit-linked insurance (CBIRC rules). Its real value is "payments lasting as long as life" and "forcing you not to touch the money," suiting those who already have base protection and want to lock in some lifetime cash flow. If you look only at return, long-run compounding lags index funds—you "trade return for certainty."
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission (2020). *Notice on Strengthening Life Insurance Actuarial Supervision* (regulating annuity/universal illustration rates). <https://www.cbirc.gov.cn/>; People's Bank of China et al. (2018). *Guiding Opinions on Regulating Asset Management Business of Financial Institutions* (Asset Management Rules, break rigid redemption). <https://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2022/0101/c1004-32322377.html>
- Note: [Contested] Before buying, use the "guaranteed benefits," not the "illustrated benefits," to compute the IRR; early surrender loses big—use spare money for medium-long allocation, and secure medical/critical-illness protection first, then talk about wealth-type.

### 15. Trusts / private funds (non-standard liquidity risk)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: High threshold (often 1 million yuan up) + long lock-up (1–3 years or more) + opacity + default/extension risk.
- In plain terms: Non-standard "high yield" maps to "low transparency + low liquidity." When it really goes wrong, you're last in line. A high threshold doesn't mean safe—it's often where the information gap is widest.
- Payoff: Possibly higher returns (historical non-standard fixed income once reached 7%–10%), but defaults, extension, and opaque net value are frequent. After the Asset Management Rules (2018) banned pooling, broke rigid redemption, and eliminated multi-layer nesting, the old implicit guarantee of "trust/private = sure redemption" vanished; post-2018 real-estate trust and LGFV non-standard troubles (e.g., 2020–2022 multiple developers' debt defaults dragging trust plans into extension) exposed non-standard's real credit risk. Private securities may have long-run alpha, but screening cost is huge, with liquidation/drawdown risk alongside. For ordinary people, non-standard's high yield mostly comes from "liquidity premium + credit downgrade," not skill premium.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: People's Bank of China and four other ministries (2018). *Guiding Opinions on Regulating Asset Management Business of Financial Institutions* (ban pooling, break rigid redemption, eliminate multi-layer nesting). <https://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2022/0101/c1004-32322377.html>; Securities Times (2023). "Behind Real-Estate Trust Extensions: Investor Suitability Management Still Needs Strengthening" (2021 real-estate trust defaults 91.7 billion yuan, 2022 93.0 billion yuan). <https://stcn.com/article/detail/1176465.html>; China Economic Net (2022). "Real-Estate Trust 'Great Collapse'" (defaults over 20 billion yuan since 2021). <https://finance.ce.cn/stock/gsgdbd/202208/18/t20220818_38037408.shtml>
- Note: [Contested] Top-quality private funds do have long-run alpha, but threshold, screening, and due-diligence cost are huge and ordinary people more easily step on landmines; non-standard "high yield" is essentially compensation for liquidity and credit risk—don't compare it with deposits/wealth products.

### 16. Increasing whole-life / long-term-care insurance (judge by IRR as wealth)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Long lock-up (early surrender loses hugely, cash value far below premiums paid in early years) + fees (death/long-term-care coverage cost).
- In plain terms: It's essentially a "very long-term, low-return, hard-locked" savings policy. Fine as protection or estate planning; buying it as high-return wealth will lose on liquidity.
- Payoff: Certain long-run cash-value growth, suited as a "guaranteed ballast," but IRR is very low in early years and about 2%–3% long-run. Regulators have repeatedly cut ordinary life insurance's predetermined rate (from 3.5% to 3.0% and below), capping long-run compounding; cash value rises yearly by contract, written in black and white, highly certain, but early surrender commonly loses 30%–50%. Its strength is "locking long-run rates + designated inheritance + debt isolation (where applicable)," not return—as wealth it lags index funds and treasury combinations long-run, suiting only the extremely risk-averse who need forced saving and targeted payout.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission (2023). Regulatory requirement to lower ordinary life insurance's predetermined rate (rate moving down). <https://www.cbirc.gov.cn/>; People's Bank of China et al. (2018). *Guiding Opinions on Regulating Asset Management Business of Financial Institutions* (Asset Management Rules, break rigid redemption). <https://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2022/0101/c1004-32322377.html>
- Note: [Contested] Before buying, evaluate by "long-run IRR," not "coverage growth"; early surrender loses big, and after the rate cut returns fall further—pair with treasuries/index funds rather than replace them.

### 17. Futures (high-leverage zero-sum)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Leverage (often 5–20x) + rollover cost + large margin top-up + daily mark-to-market forced-liquidation risk.
- In plain terms: Futures are hedging tools for professionals, not a retail ATM. At 10x leverage, a 10% adverse move wipes you out. Ordinary people touching futures basically feed the market margin.
- Payoff: Theoretically two-way profit, but negative-sum for retail: at high leverage a small swing blows up the account, mostly net loss long-run. The CSRC's *Measures for the Administration of Investor Suitability for Securities and Futures* (Order No. 130) and the financial-futures suitability rules state that opening a financial-futures account requires available margin-account funds ≥ 500,000 yuan, plus a knowledge test and trading-experience review—precisely because it is "highly professional, high-leverage, high-risk." The CFFEX's *Implementation Measures for the Financial Futures Investor Suitability System* institutionalizes leverage and suitability thresholds; CSRC investor-education material notes futures' "small-controls-large" leverage makes risk far above stocks, and a wrong direction loses on both up and down moves. It's fundamentally a risk-management tool; retail naked long/short is mostly a net drain.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China Securities Regulatory Commission (2017). *Measures for the Administration of Investor Suitability for Securities and Futures* (CSRC Order No. 130; Articles 16/17 high-leverage product risk grade). <https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100210/c1498841/1498841/files/1636094382980_55189.pdf>; China Financial Futures Exchange (2013). *Implementation Measures for the Financial Futures Investor Suitability System* (Article 4 500,000-yuan threshold, knowledge test and experience requirements). <http://www.cffex.com.cn/u/cms/www/oldsys/W020130830532922638511.pdf>; CSRC Tianjin Regulatory Bureau (2020). Investor Protection Education (futures) (leverage and daily-settlement risk). <https://www.csrc.gov.cn/tianjin/c105377/c1263609/content.shtml>
- Note: [Contested] Futures are a hedging necessity for industrial clients; for retail without a hedge underlying and strict stop-loss discipline, the long-run expectation is negative. Ordinary people should default to "stay away"; if you truly need it, open a sim account to practice first.

### 18. Foreign-exchange margin (stay away)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: High leverage (often 100x+) + black-platform risk + two-way exchange rate + principal may go to zero and isn't legally protected.
- In plain terms: Nine of ten "teachers taking you to trade forex" in your friend circle are black platforms. Legal channels are already restricted; if you can easily open a forex account, it's probably set up to swallow your principal. Default to not touching it.
- Payoff: Almost no positive expectation. SAFE and other departments state clearly: the PBOC, CBIRC, CSRC, and SAFE and their branches have approved no institution to conduct or agent foreign-exchange margin business in China; all online forex platforms are illegal in China, organizers are prosecuted and participants unprotected (SAFE risk alert). Per the *Notice on Severely Investigating Illegal Foreign-Exchange Futures and Foreign-Exchange Margin Trading* (Zhengjianfazi [1994] No. 165), conducting forex margin trading without approval is illegal; overseas platforms are mostly bucket shops / can't withdraw; in a typical case someone was sentenced for illegally absorbing tens of millions of yuan under "managed trading, 24% annualized" (SAFE / Guangming Net). For domestic residents it's not just negative expectation—it's illegal with no recourse.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (2023). "No Protection for Funds Traded: Beware of Online 'Forex Trading'" (no forex margin business approved in China). <https://m.gmw.cn/2023-03/09/content_1303304812.htm>; State Administration of Foreign Exchange (2019). Risk alert on preventing online forex investment and trading platforms (basis Zhengjianfazi [1994] No. 165). <http://m.safe.gov.cn/big5/big5/www.safe.gov.cn/fujian/2019/1120/1033.html>; China Securities Network (2019). "Beware of Online Forex Platforms, All Illegally 'Naked'." <https://www.cnstock.com/commonDetail/93214>
- Note: [Contested] There is no gray zone of "legal domestic forex margin"; any domestic solicitation of forex trading is illegal, participants are unprotected, should be blocked outright—don't enter for "high returns."

### 19. Public FOF / advisory (lazy allocation)
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Advisory fee / FOF management fee (an extra layer of 0.3%–1%/year; advisory account asset-net-value annual fee capped at 5%).
- In plain terms: If you're too lazy to allocate and afraid of stepping on landmines, a FOF or a licensed adviser is "paying for allocation discipline." See the fees clearly; don't pay multiple layers of management fee just for convenience.
- Payoff: Professionals do stock-bond allocation and rebalancing—good for those who don't want to manage it themselves and fear buying wrong; cost buys peace of mind. In Oct 2019 the CSRC issued the *Notice on Piloting Public Fund Investment-Advisory Business*; Huaxia, E Fund, Harvest, Southern, and China Universal were the first pilot managers, launching the "buy-side advisory" model—charging on account assets, deciding subscriptions/redemptions on the client's behalf, aiming to curb chase-the-rally and align intermediary with investor interests (China Securities Network). A FOF achieves diversification through a basket of funds. Its long-run return depends on the underlying assets (mostly stock-bond fund mixes); it creates no alpha itself—the core value is "discipline + fit," worth it for the weak-willed and time-poor, but a skippable fee for those who can discipline themselves to dollar-cost average.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: China Securities Regulatory Commission (2019). *Notice on Piloting Public Fund Investment-Advisory Business* (first 5 pilots, advisory fee ≤5% annualized). <https://www.cs.com.cn/xwzx/201910/t20191026_5992823.html>; The Paper / China Fund News (2019). "Fund Investment-Advisory Business Pilot Formally Launched." <https://m.thepaper.cn/baijiahao_4782317>
- Note: [Contested] Advisory/FOF solves "behavior and fit," not "high return"; compare total fees with underlying fund fees, beware multi-layer fees eroding returns, choose licensed institutions and read the strategy and conflict-of-interest clauses.

### 20. Interbank CD index fund (cash upgrade)
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Almost zero (low risk, high liquidity, no subscription/redemption fee); only tiny rate swings and opportunity cost.
- In plain terms: If your emergency/short money wants an extra half point and you dare not buy volatile products, the interbank CD index fund is a "micro-upgrade" of the money market fund. Still cash management, not investment.
- Payoff: A bit higher than money market funds (about 1.5%–2.5%, tens of basis points above in good times), still very low risk, good for parking large emergency funds or short idle cash. It mainly tracks an AAA-rated interbank CD index; the underlying is bank interbank CDs (the *Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Money Market Funds* lists interbank CDs as investable for money market funds), short duration, high credit rating, far smaller net-value swing than short-bond funds but slightly larger than money market funds (money funds use amortized cost, CD funds mostly fair value, with small drawdowns on extreme days). After the 2022 cash-product rules, it forms the "cash-management trio" with money funds and cash wealth products, ranked low-to-high by return/liquidity/risk: money fund ≈ cash wealth < CD index fund < short-bond fund.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: China Securities Regulatory Commission and the People's Bank of China (2015). *Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Money Market Funds* (CSRC Order No. 120; Article 4 interbank CDs investable). <https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c106256/c1654015/1654015/files/52c60ebbeb5642e08f9f629bb8ed190e.pdf>; People's Bank of China and CBIRC (2021). *Notice on Regulating Cash-Management Wealth Products* (cash wealth aligned with money funds, yields trending down). <https://jrb.changsha.gov.cn/jr/202201/t20220110_10433968.htm>
- Note: [Contested] CD index funds yield slightly more but are fair-valued and can have tiny single-day drawdowns—unsuitable for the "absolutely must not lose" emergency floor; truly "need-it-anytime" money still prefers money market/demand deposits, with CD funds for "short (1 week–3 month) idle cash."
