# XI. A-Share Practical Playbook

This chapter covers only the "money at the rule level" of the A-share market—trading rules, taxes, fees, IPO subscription, delisting, leverage, and cross-market access. They aren't glamorous, but each one is a high-certainty "win-rate adjuster," and all are anchored to official documents and exchange rules. Many people think losing at stock trading comes from "picking the wrong stock," but the SSE's annual Shanghai Securities Market Statistical Yearbook shows retail investors (natural-person investors) had net outflows across bull-bear cycles while institutions had net inflows; one important reason is the tax-and-fee friction from frequent trading, chasing rallies and selling dips, and misuse of the rules (price limits, delisting, leverage). Master the "deterministic rules" in this chapter—it won't guarantee you make money, but it can cut the "money you're sure to lose" by more than half.

Before placing any order, first build a panoramic map of A-share trading rules: ① Settlement and turnaround—A-shares use T+1 (buy today, sell tomorrow), but Stock Connect targets use T+0 turnaround and T+2 settlement; ② Price limits—main board (SSE/SZSE main board) ±10%, risk-warning board (ST/*ST) only ±5%, STAR Market and ChiNext ±20% with no price limit for the first 5 trading days of new listings, Beijing Stock Exchange (BSE) ±30% with no limit on the first day; ③ Board thresholds—STAR Market and BSE require "average daily assets of 500,000 yuan over the prior 20 trading days + 2 years of trading experience," ChiNext requires "100,000 yuan + 2 years," and none of these include margin-financed funds; ④ Dragon-and-tiger board (top tradelist)—exchanges publish the top 5 brokerage branches buying and selling abnormal-movement stocks daily (main board triggers at ±7% deviation, turnover ≥20%, or amplitude ≥15%; the dual-innovation boards have higher thresholds); it's a window into main-force capital, but it discloses only the top 5 seats with a lag—blindly "following the board" can easily be lured into pump-and-dump by hot money splitting positions. These rules determine "what you can buy, how it moves, and when you exit."

This chapter's entries follow the six-field structure "Cost — In plain terms — Payoff — Evidence — Sources — Note." The Payoff sections are written thick (with concrete numbers, regulatory years, and clauses); sources give verifiable official or authoritative-media links, and evidence grades A/B/C mark credibility. Treat it as a "rule-based money-saving manual": change what you can (brokerage commission waiver of the 5-yuan minimum, fund dividend method, choosing A vs C share classes) immediately; for what you can't change (stamp duty, transfer fee, delisting red lines), use them to discipline your own behavior. Below, the twelve entries are grouped by theme for easy reference.

This section's entries are grouped by theme (entry numbers in parentheses):
- Tax-and-fee cost category (rigid friction at the rule level): Entry 1 (securities trading stamp duty), Entry 2 (dividend income tax), Entry 3 (brokerage commission and the "no-5-yuan-minimum" trap), Entry 4 (total trading friction);

### 1. Securities trading stamp duty: 0.05% unilateral (halved in 2023)
<!-- tags: money=little time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: You pay securities trading stamp duty at 0.05% of the transaction amount when you sell. Per the Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration Announcement No. 39 of 2023, from August 28, 2023 it was halved from the original 0.1% to 0.05%; further back, from September 19, 2008 it changed from bilateral collection to unilateral collection from the seller (transferor) only, with no charge on the buy side. Its legal framework is the Stamp Tax Law of the People's Republic of China (adopted June 10, 2021, effective July 1, 2022), Article 3: "securities trading stamp duty is levied on the transferor of securities transactions, not on the transferee," and the attached tax-item rate table clearly sets the securities transaction tax rate at 0.1% (1‰) of the transaction amount; after the halving the effective rate is 0.05%.
- In plain terms: The only tax you can't avoid when buying and selling stocks. After the 2023 halving, every 10,000 yuan of stock you sell costs 5 yuan in duty. It only bites the sell side—every time you sell, you first lose 0.05%, regardless of how much you bought or at what price.
- Payoff: This is a "certain loss" that won't disappear because of your stock-picking skill. Take 100,000 yuan principal, fully rotating the position about once a month on average over the year (about 12 sells a year): stamp duty alone costs roughly 100,000 × 0.05% × 12 = 600 yuan a year, about 0.6% of principal. If you trade more frequently, with an annual turnover rate of 1000% (annual buy-and-sell volume about 10× the position's market value, sell volume about 5× the market value), unilateral stamp duty alone eats about 0.05% × 5 = 0.25% of principal; adding commission and transfer fee, the annual friction cost can reach 1%–2%. Over a 30-year compounding horizon, stably losing 1% a year in fees leaves a final value about a quarter lower than the "zero-friction" scenario ((1.01)^30 ≈ 1.35, equivalent to about 26% of potential returns quietly evaporating). Making fewer meaningless small trades is the most direct way to thicken returns.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration Announcement No. 39 of 2023, "Announcement on Halving the Securities Trading Stamp Duty." <https://szs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202308/t20230827_3904226.htm>; Stamp Tax Law of the People's Republic of China (2021), Article 3 and the attached tax-item rate table. <https://www.szse.cn/www/lawrules/rules/law/P020220927404715268709.pdf>
- Note: Evidence grade A (statutory provision, directly executable). Rationale: the tax rate and collection method both come from current law and ministry announcements. Limitation: stamp duty is a macro-control tool, adjusted multiple times historically (2008 switch to unilateral, 2023 halving), and may still change in the future. Cross-reference: together with Entries 3 and 4 it forms the "trading friction" trio; the SSE Shanghai Securities Market Statistical Yearbook shows retail investors overall underperform due to frequent trading, partly because of this kind of rigid friction (see other chapters of this book).

### 2. Dividend income tax: holding over 1 year is tax-free
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: To get "tax-free dividends," you must hold for more than 1 year. Per Cai Shui [2015] No. 101, for individuals obtaining shares of listed companies from the public offering market, if the holding period exceeds 1 year, the dividend income is temporarily exempt from individual income tax; if held 1 month or less (inclusive), taxed in full; held more than 1 month to 1 year (inclusive), temporarily halved to 50% of taxable income; the above income is uniformly taxed at 20%. The effective tax burden is thus: ≤1 month 20%, 1 month–1 year 10%, >1 year 0%.
- In plain terms: The state uses tax to encourage you to hold long-term. The same dividend: hold 20 days and pay 20% tax, hold 400 days and pay 0. Dividend / high-yield strategy investors must nail this rule—don't let the dividend you earned get eaten by tax.
- Payoff: For dividend strategies it's a "free" spread. Suppose a stock pays 0.5 yuan per share annually, and you hold 10,000 shares (cost 100,000 yuan), annual dividend 5,000 yuan: if sold within 1 month of holding, tax 1,000 yuan (20%); if sold at 1–12 months, tax 500 yuan (10%); if sold after holding a full year, tax 0 yuan. Holding a high-dividend portfolio long-term, on tax alone you can raise the after-tax dividend yield from 4.0% to 5.0% (at a 5% pre-tax dividend yield), equivalent to 25% more cash return without moving the stock price. Note the holding period is calculated on a "first-in-first-out" basis; the record date and the day before transfer-delivery are key nodes, and selling within the holding period is taxed by the days already held.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration, CSRC, Cai Shui [2015] No. 101, "Notice on Issues Concerning the Differentiated Individual Income Tax Policy for Dividends of Listed Companies." <https://www.chinatax.gov.cn/chinatax/n810341/n810765/n1465977/201510/c1967339/content.html>; State Taxation Administration 12366, "How Individual Dividends from Listed Companies Are Taxed under Individual Income Tax." <https://12366.chinatax.gov.cn/sscx/detail?code=0000000002013050300023>
- Note: Evidence grade A. Rationale: directly cites a joint document of the Ministry of Finance, the tax authority, and the CSRC. Limitation: this preferential treatment applies only to dividends from "A-share listed company stocks"; the holding period is counted in calendar days and on a "first-in-first-out" basis per securities account; the National Equities Exchange and Quotations (NEEQ, the "New Third Board") follows the same. Cross-reference: complements the tax-free rules of Entry 12 (fund dividends).

### 3. Brokerage commission and the "no-5-yuan-minimum" trap
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Commission is agreed between broker and client; under regulatory guidance the industry generally charges by transaction amount, with the common "5 yuan minimum per order" (orders under 5 yuan in commission are charged 5 yuan); there is also a transfer fee. To get "no-5" (waiving the 5-yuan floor) you usually need to actively negotiate with the broker or choose an internet broker / special channel, and "no-5" is not offered by every broker.
- In plain terms: Many brokers default to a 5-yuan minimum per order; you buy 1,000 yuan of stock and still pay 5 yuan—a 0.5% hidden cost. Negotiate "no-5" and it's calculated at a fraction of a percent. Small-amount dollar-cost-averaging and grid-trading folks should especially watch this—the more you split your buys, the more the 5-yuan minimum hurts.
- Payoff: When buying in small batches, what "no-5" saves is pure profit. Example: monthly DCA of 3,000 yuan, split into 3 orders of 1,000 yuan each; at "5-yuan minimum" you'd pay 15 yuan commission (0.5% of principal), 18 orders a year totaling 270 yuan; if you negotiate 0.015% (1.5 bp) with no-5, annual commission is only 3,000 × 12 × 0.015% = 5.4 yuan. The gap is about 50×. Adding the transfer fee (Shanghai and Shenzhen A-shares lowered from 0.02‰ to 0.01‰ bilateral, i.e., one-tenth of a basis point bilateral, effective April 29, 2022), the small-amount cost structure improves significantly. For retail investors trading dozens of times a year via DCA / quant-style, "no-5" alone saves hundreds to over a thousand yuan a year, and this saving goes straight to returns, untaxed.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: China Economic Net (2022), "Stimulating Market Vitality: Transfer Fee Halved from Today" (discloses brokerage commission capped at 3‰ of transaction amount, orders under 5 yuan charged 5 yuan). <http://m.ce.cn/cj/202204/29/t20220429_37542199.shtml>; People's Daily Online (2022), "China Clearing: Stock Trading Transfer Fee Lowered 50% Overall" (A-share transfer fee unified at 0.01‰ bilateral). <https://finance.people.com.cn/BIG5/n1/2022/0428/c1004-32411381.html>
- Note: Evidence grade B. Rationale: the specific commission rate is agreed between broker and client per industry practice; the regulator sets a cap and the "5-yuan minimum" brokerage convention; whether "no-5" is negotiable depends on broker policy. Limitation: varies greatly across brokers and client asset sizes—don't treat "no-5" as universal. Cross-reference: same trading-friction cost as Entries 1 and 4.

### 4. Total trading friction: stamp duty + commission + transfer fee
<!-- tags: money=little time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: The rigid friction of one complete buy-sell = unilateral stamp duty 0.05% (sell side only) + bilateral commission (generally from 0.854 bp, negotiable to 1–1.5 bp, with the "5-yuan minimum") + bilateral transfer fee (A-share 0.01‰, i.e., one-tenth of a basis point, Shanghai-Shenzhen bilateral). Added up, a single round-trip costs about 0.1%–0.2%.
- In plain terms: Every buy-sell looks like "no money spent," but you actually get bitten by friction first. The more you churn, the deeper the bite—this is the hard-cost source of certain losses from frequent trading, independent of whether you picked right.
- Payoff: Seeing the friction magnitude lets you control your hands. Estimate at an annual turnover rate of 1000% (annual volume about 10× average position market value, of which sell volume about 5× market value): stamp duty 0.05% × 5 = 0.25%, commission bilateral about 0.015% × 10 = 0.15%, transfer fee 0.001% × 10 = 0.01%, totaling about 0.4%/year; if turnover doubles to 2000%, friction reaches around 0.8%, and at high frequency (>3000%) it exceeds 1%. These costs are "deducted before you earn"—you must first beat friction in stock-picking before talking net return. Combined with Entries 1 and 3, a conservative investor who keeps annual turnover under 200% retains 0.5–1 percentage point more principal a year versus a high-frequency trader, a meaningful long-run compounding (over 30 years the final-value gap can exceed 20%).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration Announcement No. 39 of 2023, "Announcement on Halving the Securities Trading Stamp Duty." <https://szs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202308/t20230827_3904226.htm>; People's Daily Online (2022), "China Clearing: Stock Trading Transfer Fee Lowered 50% Overall." <https://finance.people.com.cn/BIG5/n1/2022/0428/c1004-32411381.html>; China Economic Net (2022), "Stimulating Market Vitality: Transfer Fee Halved from Today." <http://m.ce.cn/cj/202204/29/t20220429_37542199.shtml>
- Note: Evidence grade B. Rationale: stamp duty and transfer fee are public standards; commission varies by client, estimated at common industry ranges. Limitation: actual cost floats with commission negotiation, trade frequency, and order size; this is an order-of-magnitude reference, not an actuarial value. Cross-reference: with Entries 1 and 3, quantifies "the more you trade, the more you lose."

### 5. IPO subscription rules: market-value threshold + credit subscription
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: A-share IPO subscription uses "market-value subscription, credit subscription"—no upfront payment needed, but you must hold corresponding-market non-restricted-share market value (Shanghai and Shenzhen counted separately) to get quota: Shanghai calculates by the average daily held market value over the 20 trading days before T-2, every 10,000 yuan of market value entitles 1,000 shares (one subscription unit), amounts under 10,000 yuan are not counted (Shanghai Securities Issue [2025] No. 43). After winning, pay on T+2. Note: subscribing to STAR Market and ChiNext new shares also requires first opening the corresponding board trading permission (STAR Market 500k + 2 years, ChiNext 100k + 2 years); BSE subscription likewise requires BSE permission.
- In plain terms: You don't need extra cash for "subscription eligibility"—if your account holds stock market value, you get quota. But don't load up on high-risk stocks just to meet the market-value threshold—that's putting the cart before the horse. Winning means earning the price gap; it's essentially a "free lottery."
- Payoff: Participate on top of your existing holdings; winning earns the gap between issue price and listing price. Of the 100 A-share IPOs listed in 2024, only 1 broke issue on the first day; in the first half of 2025, all 45 new shares rose on the first day (break-issue rate about 0%), and a single winning lot often yields several thousand to tens of thousands of yuan (e.g., Changlian Tech in 2024 had a per-lot paper gain of nearly 180,000 yuan). But under the registration-based IPO system there's no longer "guaranteed profit": STAR Market and ChiNext new shares have no price limit for the first 5 trading days, main board new shares also open up in the first 5 days (after which main board ±10%, dual-innovation ±20%, BSE ±30%), with extreme first-day volatility, break-issues and surges coexisting. You must "pick and subscribe"—avoid high issue P/E ("three highs") targets, not subscribe blindly; also remember the market-value threshold is "hold shares to get quota," loading up on junk stocks just for subscription turns a low-risk lottery into a high-risk bet.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Shanghai Stock Exchange and China Securities Depository and Clearing Corporation, "Implementation Rules for Online Issuance of Initial Public Offerings in the Shanghai Market (Revised March 2025)" (Shanghai Securities Issue [2025] No. 43, Articles 3 and 4). <https://neris.csrc.gov.cn/falvfagui/rdqsHeader/mainbody?navbarId=1&secFutrsLawId=2c69d1c9563f499fb51deb51259445a4>; Shanghai Stock Exchange, "One Thing for Stock Investment: IPO Subscription" (participation conditions, T+2 payment). <https://one.sse.com.cn/onething/gptz>; Shenzhen Stock Exchange, "Implementation Measures for Investor Suitability Management of the ChiNext (Revised 2020)" (100k + 2 years). <https://investor.szse.cn/knowledge/t20200522_577479.html>
- Note: Evidence grade A. Rationale: market-value threshold, T+2 payment, and board permissions are all backed by exchange rules. Limitation: subscription returns swing widely with issuance pace and pricing environment; the 2022 break-issue rate once reached 28%; "free lottery" is not risk-free. Cross-reference: with Entry 6 (break-issue rate), Entry 7 (delisting risk), Entry 10 (convertible bond subscription) forms the "IPO family."

### 6. Break-issue rate changes: from "guaranteed profit" to "pick and subscribe"
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: The time cost of studying issue valuation and picking subscriptions—focus on whether the issue P/E / valuation is high ("three highs" issuance), industry sentiment, and float size; avoid clearly expensive new shares.
- In plain terms: After the registration-based system, "new shares never lose" was broken. Now you watch the issue valuation: don't subscribe to expensive ones, subscribe to cheap ones. Treating it as stable income no longer holds; treating it as a "free lottery" is fine.
- Payoff: See the cycle with data: per Securities Times statistics, A-share first-day break-issue rate was about 4.2% in 2021, reached 28.27% in 2022 (high year), 16.61% in 2023, dropped to about 3% in 2024 (of 100 shares for the year, only 1 broke), and in the first half of 2025, 45 shares had zero break-issues. Behind this is the fall in issue P/E (comparable average about 49.9× in 2022 → about 22× in 2025) and tightening of IPO pace. Lesson for retail: the break-issue rate rises and falls with pricing heat—don't carve the boat to find the lost sword; but "blind subscription" cost many people several thousand yuan per lot in 2022. In practice, prioritize low-valuation, small-float, main-board / BSE targets, and avoid "three highs" new shares whose issue P/E is far above the industry average; after winning, if the first-day gain is too large, consider taking profit rather than holding hard—treat subscription as a probability game, not a guaranteed-return product.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Securities Times (2024), "Huge Change! New-Share Gains Double, Is Blind Subscription Back?" (2022 break-issue rate 28.27%, 2023 16.61%, 2024 about 3%). <https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/1167700.html>; Sina Finance (2025), "Zero Break-Issues, Back to the Blind-Subscription Era" (45 zero break-issues in H1 2025, 2022 break-issue rate >28%). <https://finance.sina.cn/cj/2025-06-09/detail-ineznnvk9863243.d.html>
- Note: Evidence grade B. Rationale: the break-issue rate is media statistics based on Wind / exchange data, not a regulatory provision, and varies by sample window. Limitation: different statistical scopes (whether including BSE, STAR Market) cause number differences; here is an approximation for the main board + dual-innovation scope. Cross-reference: with Entry 5 (subscription rules), Entry 7 (registration-based delisting) jointly shows "new shares ≠ guaranteed profit."

### 7. Delisting and ST: the risk of permanent principal loss
<!-- tags: money=0 time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: The cost of judging in a few minutes "whether you're stepping on a landmine"—watch the four types of mandatory delisting red lines (trading-based, financial, normative, and major-violation) and the risk-warning (ST/*ST) markers. Trading-based: closing price below 1 yuan for 20 consecutive trading days (face-value delisting), or total market value below 500 million yuan for 20 consecutive trading days (SSE), or failing volume / shareholder-count thresholds (SSE Article 9.2.1). Financial (revised 2024, effective January 1, 2025): most recent fiscal year "net profit (lower of pre/post extraordinary items) negative AND operating revenue below 300 million yuan (main board) / 100 million yuan (STAR Market, ChiNext)," negative net assets, or audit issued disclaimer / adverse opinion. ST is other risk warning (±5% price limit), *ST is delisting risk warning.
- In plain terms: After A-share delisting became normalized, trading ST stocks and gambling on restructuring is licking blood off a knife edge. Cheap ≠ a bargain—if it's continuously loss-making, has negative net assets, or got a non-standard audit opinion, avoid it first. Once trading-based delisting triggers, there's no delisting cleanup period—it's directly delisted.
- Payoff: Avoiding delisted stocks means avoiding permanent principal loss. Typical cases: in 2024, *ST Tongda was *ST'd because its 2022 net profit was negative and revenue below 100 million yuan, and its 2023 annual report got a disclaimer opinion, on which the SSE terminated its listing; in 2025, *ST Yuancheng had closing prices below 1 yuan for multiple consecutive days and total market value below 500 million yuan, hitting trading-based mandatory delisting. Delisted stocks commonly fall 70%–90% or even to zero, with abruptly dried-up liquidity and slim restructuring hope. The most practical discipline for retail: ① don't touch stocks already flagged (ST/*ST) without fundamental reversal; ② immediately be alert when price approaches 1 yuan and market value approaches the 500-million-yuan warning line; ③ avoid in the quarter a financial report gets a non-standard opinion. Treat "not stepping on a landmine" as a bigger win-lose lever than "catching a bull stock."
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: CSRC Ningxia Regulatory Bureau (2025), "What Are Trading-Based Mandatory Delistings (SSE Edition)" (Article 9.2.1: 1-yuan face value / 500-million-yuan market value, etc.). <https://www.csrc.gov.cn/ningxia/c104435/c7544159/content.shtml>; Shanghai Stock Exchange, "Announcement on Terminating the Listing of Shanghai Tongda Venture Capital Co., Ltd." (financial delisting example). <https://www.sse.com.cn/disclosure/announcement/listing/c/c_20240528_10758046.shtml>; Shanghai Stock Exchange, "Announcement on Lifting the Normative Delisting Risk Warning and Continuing to Implement the Financial Delisting Risk Warning and Other Risk Warning" (*ST Xintong, revenue below 300 million / non-standard audit). <https://www.sse.com.cn/disclosure/listedinfo/announcement/c/new/2025-03-22/600289_20250322_SN4M.pdf>
- Note: Evidence grade A. Rationale: delisting indicators come from Shanghai-Shenzhen Listing Rules and regulatory Q&As, and cases are official exchange announcements. Limitation: main board and dual-innovation have different financial revenue thresholds (300 million vs 100 million), and rules adjust dynamically with "State Council Nine-Point Guidelines" revisions (revised 2024, effective 2025). Cross-reference: with Entries 5 and 6 jointly flags the "wash-out" risk under the registration-based system; ST/*ST ±5% price limit is also part of the trading rules.

### 8. Margin trading and securities lending: leverage's legal cloak
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self+family -->
- Cost: Borrowing from a broker to trade stocks costs interest (about 5%–8% annualized, floating with credit rating and collateral) + maintenance guarantee ratio constraints. By rule, maintenance guarantee ratio = (cash + securities market value in credit account) / (financing debt + interest expense); a 2024 CSRC press briefing disclosed the all-market average maintenance guarantee ratio is about 226%, the typical minimum liquidation line is agreed at 130%, warning line about 150%, margin-call period generally 1–2 trading days, and below the agreed ratio without topping up triggers forced liquidation. Since 2019 the uniform "liquidation line no lower than 130%" restriction has been removed, left to broker-client agreement.
- In plain terms: Margin trading is "borrowing money / securities from a broker to trade stocks." In a bull market it feels better; in a bear market it forces you to sell at the floor price—and when it drops to the liquidation line you may not even get the chance to add margin. Ordinary people default to not using it.
- Payoff: Leverage theoretically amplifies gains, but losses amplify equally on the downside, and many A-share boards have ±20% (dual-innovation) or ±30% (BSE) daily limits, so single-day swings easily breach the guarantee ratio. More fatal is "passive exit": when your account is force-liquidated at the bottom, you bear the actual loss and lose the chance to recover in the subsequent rebound—turning "temporary paper loss" into "permanent exit." Statistically, margin balances fall fast in sharp declines via forced liquidation and active de-risking, but the liquidated accounts often lose heavily. For 99% of retail investors, margin trading's expected value is no better than their own capital—it turns "slow loss" into "instant nothing." Unless you have mature position management and extreme-market coping experience, stay away from leverage.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China Securities Regulatory Commission (2024), "CSRC Spokesperson Answers Reporters on Margin Financing Business" (average maintenance guarantee ratio 226%, liquidation line typically 130%). <https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c7461992/content.shtml>; China Securities Regulatory Commission (2019), "CSRC Guides Exchanges to Improve Margin Trading and Securities Lending Mechanisms" (removing the uniform "liquidation line no lower than 130%" restriction). <https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c1001080/content.shtml>; Changjiang Securities, "Frequently Asked Questions on Margin Trading" (extraction line 300%, warning line 140%, liquidation line 130%, margin-call period). <https://www.cjsc.com.cn/main/a/20251021/36476.shtml>
- Note: Evidence grade A. Rationale: margin rules and regulatory guidance come from CSRC public press briefings and broker business rules. Limitation: liquidation lines and rates are broker-client agreed values, varying greatly individually; the margin-call buffer period varies by broker policy. Cross-reference: with Entry 7 (delisting), Entry 4 (friction) jointly forms the "don't let yourself exit passively" theme.

### 9. Shanghai-Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock Connect (northbound / southbound): a cross-market access channel
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Participating in the Hong Kong Stock Connect requires personal securities and capital account assets totaling no less than 500,000 yuan (excluding margin-financed portions), passing the broker's suitability assessment, and signing a risk disclosure statement (Shanghai-Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock Connect business measures). It also carries currency-conversion cost and the timing / settlement differences from "T+0 turnaround, T+2 settlement."
- In plain terms: To allocate some Tencent, Meituan, you don't need a Hong Kong account—the Stock Connect works. But Hong Kong stocks swing more, have no price limits (a single stock can halve or double in a day), and use T+0 turnaround trading—don't apply A-share "price-limit protection" experience to it.
- Payoff: Buy Stock Connect targets without currency exchange, diversify away from a single market, and allocate to internet leaders. But Hong Kong stocks carry three risks A-shares don't: ① no price limit, single-day swings far exceeding A-shares; ② "shell-company scam stock" risk—some small-cap Hong Kong stocks frequently do large proportional discounted rights issues / placements, splits and consolidations, so share count and face value can change drastically; ③ Hong Kong has no delisting risk warning or delisting cleanup arrangement, stocks may be delisted directly, and the nominal-holder services of China Clear may also be limited. In 2025 southbound funds had a record full-year net inflow of about 1.4 trillion HKD, showing the Stock Connect has become a mainstream channel, but "channel convenience" ≠ "low-risk targets." Suggest using only spare money, controlling position size, and avoiding low-priced stocks with a record of heavy consolidations.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China Securities Regulatory Commission, "Shanghai-Hong Kong Stock Connect Q&A No. 6: Hong Kong Stock Connect Stocks and Investors" (personal assets ≥500,000 yuan, T+0 turnaround / T+2 settlement, no price limit). <https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100210/c1498886/content.shtml>; Shenzhen Stock Exchange, "Shenzhen-Hong Kong Stock Connect Business Implementation Measures (Revised 2024)" (Article 105: personal assets ≥500,000 yuan). <https://neris.csrc.gov.cn/falvfagui/rdqsHeader/mainbody?navbarId=1&secFutrsLawId=f93393f6d550460d8d09aa7467c09166>; CSRC Shanxi Bureau, "Basic Knowledge for Investors Participating in Hong Kong Stock Connect Trading" (no price limit, shell-company scam stocks, direct-delisting risk). <https://www.csrc.gov.cn/shanxi/c1055540/c1609328/content.shtml>
- Note: Evidence grade A. Rationale: access thresholds and trading mechanisms come from CSRC and exchange public rules. Limitation: 500,000 yuan is the individual-investor threshold, institutions aren't bound by it; the Stock Connect target scope adjusts dynamically, and "shell-company scam stocks" and direct delisting are Hong Kong-specific risks. Cross-reference: with Entry 7 ("direct delisting"), Entry 8 (leverage volatility) jointly flags "rule differences = risk."

### 10. Convertible bond subscription: a near-free "lottery," but with break-issues
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=some payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Convertible bond subscription is "credit subscription, zero market-value threshold, pay after winning"—an empty account can participate, no advance payment on T day, just keep enough funds in the account before 16:00 on T+2 (1,000 yuan per lot); but if you win and fail to pay in full 3 times cumulatively within 12 consecutive months, you'll be restricted from online subscription of new shares and bonds for 6 months (Shanghai and Shenzhen counted together). Note: for investors who opened convertible-bond permission after June 18, 2022, you also need 24 months of trading experience + average daily assets of no less than 100,000 yuan over the prior 20 days + risk rating C3 or above.
- In plain terms: Convertible bond subscription has almost zero threshold, pay after winning—a rare "win first, pay later." But don't get greedy on the first listing day—take profit and be safe; don't trade high-priced bonds like stocks, beware forced redemption.
- Payoff: Historically low break-issue rate, mostly rising on listing, but absolutely not zero-risk. Trading rules: convertible bonds are T+0, with first-day price limit +57.3% / −43.3% (i.e., max 157.3 yuan, min 56.7 yuan), ±20% from the next day; third-party statistics show about 97% of new bonds rose on the first day and 61% did worse on the second day than the first, so "sell on day one" is a common strategy. Risk points: ① break-issue (mostly concentrated in low-rated, niche industries, single-lot loss usually <50 yuan); ② forced redemption—triggered when the underlying stock stays ≥130% of the conversion price for at least 15 of 30 consecutive days, redemption price only about 103 yuan, far below market price; if you don't sell / convert in time you'll take a big discount; ③ default (underlying delisting or issuer cash-flow exhaustion). For retail, convertible bond subscription is a very cost-effective "free lottery," but treat "pick-and-subscribe + sell on day one + guard against forced redemption" as discipline.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: China Investor website, "Convertible Bonds" investor education (credit subscription, T+2 payment, abandonment penalty). <https://www.investor.org.cn/xxzx/tjzl/tjnrgmjytx/bk/kj/jyxl_3803/202311/P020231123553040874862.pdf>; Sina Finance, "Convertible Bond Subscription Calendar" (first day +57.3% / −43.3%, next day ±20%, T+0, 3 abandonments in 12 months limit for 6 months). <https://sina.cn/news/detail/5346220555896770.html>; Koufu Wencai, convertible bond subscription rules (new investors after 2022-06-18 need 24 months + 100k assets + risk C3). <https://licai.cofool.com/ask/vipqa_7626253_43251589.html>
- Note: Evidence grade B. Rationale: T+0, price limits, abandonment penalty, forced redemption are exchange / association public rules, but "97% break-issue rate" is third-party statistics that change with the market. Limitation: convertible bond prices are affected by the underlying and interest rates, high-priced bonds can draw down 30%+; the new-account threshold has been raised since 2022. ### 11. A-share / C-share funds: holding period decides which to pick
<!-- tags: money=little time=little will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: A-share funds charge a front-end subscription fee and no sales service fee; C-share funds waive the subscription fee and accrue the sales service fee daily (per the 2025 "Provisions on the Administration of Publicly Offered Securities Investment Fund Sales Fees," actively managed equity / hybrid fund sales service fee no higher than 0.4% / year, index / bond no higher than 0.2% / year). Management and custody fees are the same for both share classes.
- In plain terms: A and C shares of the same fund invest in exactly the same underlying; the only difference is "how they charge." For short-term trading pick C (no subscription fee, daily service fee); for long-term holding pick A (one subscription fee, then no more service fee). Don't get it backwards—getting it backwards wastes about 0.4% a year.
- Payoff: Picking the wrong share class is a hidden loss. Take a 100,000 yuan equity fund, with a 4-fold-discount subscription fee about 0.6%: if held <1 year, C waives the subscription fee but has a sales service fee (about 0.4% / year), while A charges a one-time 0.6% subscription fee—so C is better then; if held >1 year, per the 2025 new rules C no longer accrues the sales service fee after 1 year of holding, and A's subscription fee is already amortized over time, so the two fees converge and A may even be better; the break-even point is roughly 6 months–2 years (varies with fee rates and discounts). For DCA / long-term holders planning to hold 1–2 years or more, picking A saves about 0.5%–1% in fees; for short-term swing traders, C is more flexible. Before ordering, be sure to check the share-class code—don't default to buying C.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China Securities Journal (2020), "Know First Before Investing: Fund Investment Knowledge (Part 2)" (A/C share fee differences). <https://cs.com.cn/tzjj/tjdh/202010/t20201016_6101969.html>; China Economic Net (2026), "Fund Companies Accelerate Fee Optimization of Existing Products" (2025 "Provisions on the Administration of Publicly Offered Securities Investment Fund Sales Fees": C shares held >1 year waive sales service fee, cap 0.4% / year). <http://m.ce.cn/cj/gd/202608/t20260822_3164169.shtml>
- Note: Evidence grade A. Rationale: share-class categorization and fee rules are set by the CSRC "Sales Fee Provisions" and fund contracts. Limitation: under the 2025 new rules C-share long-term holding fees improve, the "break-even point" moves up versus old rules, and in practice you must calculate per the specific product's fee schedule. Cross-reference: with Entry 12 (fund dividends) jointly forms the "fund-end savings" combo.

### 12. Fund dividends: choose "dividend reinvestment," not cash
<!-- tags: money=0 time=0 will=no payoff=mid scope=money impact=self -->
- Cost: Change the dividend method once at account opening / subscription (the system default is often cash dividend), by handling the change at the sales institution before the close of trading on the business day before the record date.
- In plain terms: Cash dividend equals "the fund sells a bit for you then gives you cash," and buying back in costs another subscription fee. Dividend reinvestment converts directly to shares—free, continuous compounding, and this reinvestment is exempt from the subscription fee.
- Payoff: Dividend reinvestment adds shares free of subscription fee, with compounding uninterrupted. Per Cai Shui Zi [2002] No. 128, "Notice on Tax Issues Concerning Open-End Securities Investment Funds," income investors receive from fund profit distribution is temporarily exempt from individual and corporate income tax—the dividend itself is not taxed. Cash dividend realizes the shares, and if you buy back in you pay the subscription fee again and break the compounding. Example: a fund with a 3% annual dividend yield—if you manually reinvest after each cash dividend, you repeatedly bear subscription fees and easily miss investments due to delay; choosing dividend reinvestment auto-converts to shares, rolling at zero cost. But note: ① the holding period of reinvested shares restarts from the confirmation date, and redemption within 7 days still incurs a 1.5% redemption fee; ② after the dividend the net value drops ex-dividend, total assets are unchanged—it's not "extra money." For those bullish on the future with no immediate cash need, default to changing to dividend reinvestment.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: CSRC Ningxia Regulatory Bureau (2025), "Case Analysis of Fund Dividend Rule Disputes" (dividend reinvestment exempt from subscription fee, dividends temporarily tax-exempt). <https://www.csrc.gov.cn/ningxia/c104435/c7640238/content.shtml>; Ministry of Finance, State Taxation Administration, Cai Shui Zi [2002] No. 128, "Notice on Tax Issues Concerning Open-End Securities Investment Funds" (fund distribution income temporarily tax-exempt). <https://epaper.stcn.com/att/202606/03/ZQ03B061-ZN_eBook.pdf>
- Note: Evidence grade A. Rationale: dividend reinvestment exempt from subscription fee is an explicit fund-contract / regulatory requirement, and the tax exemption basis is Cai Shui Zi [2002] No. 128. Limitation: the exemption applies only to the "fund profit distribution" step; the gain from subscription-redemption price differences is still taxed per relevant rules; the default dividend method is mostly cash dividend, requiring active change. Cross-reference: with Entry 2 (stock dividend tax exemption), Entry 11 (fund fees) jointly reflects "tax and fee systems are returns."
